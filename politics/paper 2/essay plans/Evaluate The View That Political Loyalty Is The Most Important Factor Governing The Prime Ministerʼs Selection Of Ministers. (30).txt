Against: Arguments That Political Loyalty Isnʼt The Most Important Factor
Governing The Prime Ministerʼs Selection Of Ministers
On the other hand, Prime Ministers have to balance selecting political allies with balancing
different factions within their party and maintaining support from across the party.
In order to make sure their party stays united, Prime Ministers often select an ideologically
balanced cabinet which represents different factions of the party.
Keeping important members of the party who may oppose you in the cabinet is effective as
they have to sign up to collective responsibility and are therefore unable to publicly
challenge the government.
Theresa May, for example, appointed a cabinet with a balance between Brexiteers
(including Boris Johnson and David Davis) and Remainers (including Phillip Hammond and
Jeremy Hunt).
After leadership elections, Prime Ministers may also offer their failed opponents cabinet
positions. For example, Boris Johnson gave cabinet roles to Matt Hancock, Michael Gove,
Dominic Raab and Sajid Javid.

COUNTERPOINT More recently, however, Prime Ministers can be seen to have placed less
importance on this ideological balance. Popular Prime Ministers with large majorities may feel
safer appointing like-minded ministers and often clear out less supportive ministers in cabinet
reshuffles.
Boris Johnson appointed a significant number of Brexiteers and allies to his first cabinet,
with both the Home Secretary Priti Patel) and the Foreign Secretary Dominic Raab)
Brexiteers.
Liz Truss didnʼt offer Rishi Sunak a cabinet position after defeating him in the 2022
Conservative Party leadership election.
Starmerʼs first cabinet includes no members from the left faction of the Labour Party, with
Deputy Prime Minister Angela Rayner perhaps being the closest, having served under
Jeremy Corbyn.

**************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Both loyalty and ideological balance are clearly important factors which Prime Ministers
consider when selecting their ministers.
Especially in recent governments, however, loyalty has arguably been proved to be more
important, with Prime Ministers appointing many ideological allies to key positions.

Paragraph Two ➡ Competence and Experience vs Political
Loyalty
Against: Arguments That Political Loyalty Isnʼt The Most Important Factor
Governing The Prime Ministerʼs Selection Of Ministers

A key factor Prime Ministers consider when selecting their ministers is the individual
competence and experience of ministers.
Running a large and complex government department is a very difficult job and Prime
Ministers have to be confident that their ministerʼs have the experience and specialist
knowledge to run their department effectively.
Being an effective communicator in the media is also important, as Prime Ministers want
to ensure cabinet ministers donʼt embarrass the government/defend government
policies poorly.
A key example of Prime Ministers selecting their cabinet according to competence and
experience is Jeremy Hunt being brought in as Chancellor by both Liz Truss and Rishi Sunak.
Having served in the cabinet for 9 years since 2010, Hunt was seen as a safe pair of hands
after Kwasi Kwarteng was sacked and is a key reason why Sunak kept him as Chancellor
after replacing Truss as Prime Minister.
Other good examples are Rachel Reeves being made Chancellor, Yvette Cooper being made
Home Secretary and James Timpson being made Prisons Minister under Starmer.
Not only had Reeves served as Shadow Chancellor for 3 years, she previously worked for
the Bank of England.
Cooper is one of the few Labour MPs with significant government experience, having
served as a minister in Blairʼs government and in the Cabinet in Brownʼs government.
Timpson was made a peer in order to be made Prisons Minister. 10% of his companyʼs
Timpsons) workforce are former prisoners and he has long been a rehabilitation
campaigner with significant expertise and a positive reputation.
Prime Ministers also often consider the prospects of MPs becoming cabinet ministers in the
future when selecting junior ministers, as they feel a responsibility to keep the party secure and
in a good state for the future.
Liz Truss, for example, was made a junior minister by Cameron in 2012 and remained in
cabinet from 2014, enabling her rise in the party before she became leader and Prime
Minister.

**************************************************************************************************

For: Arguments That Political Loyalty Is The Most Important Factor Governing
The Prime Ministerʼs Selection Of Ministers
On the other hand, it can be argued that loyalty and political alignment is a more important
factor, as recent Prime Ministers have sometimes selected ministers who have limited

experience, yet belong to the same wing of the party.
A number of incompetent ministers have also been selected, such as Chris Grayling who
was nicknamed ‘Failing Graylingʼ due to his ineptitude as a minister, which included granting
a ferry contract to a company with no ferries when Secretary of State for Transport.
Liz Truss, for example, appointed Kwasi Kwarteng and James Cleverly to important roles, both
of whom had limited experience in government. Her cabinet was the least experienced in
modern history, with the average secretary of state having just 17 months of top level ministerial
experience.
Kwarteng was made Chancellor despite having served in the cabinet for less than 2 years.

**************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, competence and experience is a key factor in the Prime Ministerʼs selection of ministers
and one that is more important than political loyalty.
Loyalty was more important for Liz Truss, who appointed a very inexperienced cabinet. Most
other Prime Ministers, including Sunak, prioritise the competence of ministers.

Paragraph Three ➡ Direct Representation and Diversity vs
Political Loyalty
Against: Arguments That Political Loyalty Isnʼt The Most Important Factor
Governing The Prime Ministerʼs Selection Of Ministers
Especially in recent years, Prime Ministers have found it important to ensure their cabinets are
somewhat representative of the population in terms of gender and ethnicity in particular.
Blair appointed Margaret Becket as Britain's first female Foreign Secretary and Brown
appointed Jacqui Smith as the first female Home Secretary.
Boris Johnsonʼs December cabinet was very representative in terms of ethnic minorities, with 6
ethnic minority members of cabinet, two of whom Sunak and Patel) were in the key offices of
state.
11/21 members of Starmerʼs first cabinet are women including the first female Chancellor,
though just 3 members are from an ethnic minority background, a drop from Sunak and
Johnsonʼs cabinets.

**************************************************************************************************

For: Arguments That Political Loyalty Is The Most Important Factor Governing
The Prime Ministerʼs Selection Of Ministers
There are clearly limits to how much importance Prime Ministers place on diversity when
selecting their ministers, particularly in relation to gender. Politics, parliament and the
government continues to be largely dominated by white men.
The coalition was criticised for its lack of gender balance with only 4 women in its first
cabinet.
Though Prime Ministers do likely consider diversity in their selection of ministers, factors such
as political loyalty and individual competence/experience are arguably more important, as they
are key factors in enabling the Prime Minister to maintain their power and control.

**************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, diversity has certainly increased in recent years as a factor that influences the Prime
Ministerʼs selection of ministers. Both political loyalty and individual competence are arguably
more important, however.

Overall Argument
In conclusion, political loyalty is not the most important factor governing the Prime Ministerʼs
selection of ministers. Though it is certainly an important consideration and was arguably the
most important factor for Liz Truss who appointed multiple inexperienced allies, individual
competence and experience was more important for Prime Ministers before and after Truss.
Prime Ministers need to be confident that ministers have the experience and ability necessary
to run their departments effectively, in order to avoid embarrassing the government.
