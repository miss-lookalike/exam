Evaluate The View That Devolution Has Been A Success. (30) 

Introduction
Key Definitions
Devolution was included in Labourʼs 1997 election manifesto and introduced thereafter after
successful referendums in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, as well as in London.
There is also some devolution in the rest of England, in the form of city regions with metro
mayors.

**************************************************************************************************

Paragraph One ➡ Impact Of Devolution On Democracy
For: Arguments That Devolution Has Been A Success

A key argument that devolution has been a success is that it has allowed for greater democracy
and more effective local representation, with devolved bodies able to respond to the concerns
of their electorates.
This can be seen in particular in Scotland, in which the population is more left wing than in
the rest of the UK. This has led to the election of a left wing SNP government that has
introduced policies including free prescription charges, free tuition fees and a higher top
rate of income tax than in the rest of the UK.
This was only possible as the Scotland Act 1998 granted the Scottish Parliament control
over health and education policy, whilst the Scotland Act 2016 granted it significant
control over taxation.
This was also seen during COVID, when Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland and England
relaxed restrictions at different speeds.
Further, London Mayor Sadiq Khan has been able to introduce a number of policies to
improve the provision of services and promotion of equality compared to the rest of
England.
Free school meals for all primary school students in London were introduced in
September 2023. This £135m/year initiative by Labour mayor Sadiq Khan cost-of-living
pressures on families while also ensuring that no child goes hungry during the school
day.
In contrast, in the rest of England, only children from low-income families continue to
receive free school meals.
Another way in which devolution can be seen as a success in terms of democracy is that the
devolved bodies are elected using more proportional electoral systems which have greater
voter choice, leading to more representative Parliaments and a greater mix of parties.
The Scottish and Welsh Parliaments are elected using the Additional Member System
AMS, whilst the Northern Ireland Parliament is elected using the Single Transferrable Vote
System STV.
Devolved body leaders can generate significant personal support and legitimacy if they are
judged to effectively represent their city/devolved area and improve policy.
This can be seen in the May 2024 Mayoral elections, when Labour mayors Sadiq Khan and
Andy Burnham were both reelected for third terms, in London and Greater Manchester
respectively.
Burnham won with 63.4% of the vote, with his closest competitor getting just over 10%
of the vote. Burnham has been praised as a mayor for his effective representation of
Manchester nationally, as well as a country-leading approach to tackling homelessness
and improvement in the cityʼs transport.

**************************************************************************************************

Against: Arguments That Devolution Hasnʼt Been A Success
Whilst devolution does deliver effective local representation, it can be argued that this creates
inequality among UK citizens, as divergences in policy mean that different citizens have
different access to healthcare, education etc and are subject to different laws.
Fiscal (tax) devolution can also be argued to undermine redistribution of resources from
richer to poorer areas, therefore preventing inequality from being tackled.
Whilst devolved bodies are elected using more proportional electoral systems, turnout in
elections is generally low, which undermines the legitimacy of devolved assemblies.
Turnout was just 63.5% in the 2021 Scottish Parliament elections, the same in the 2022
Northern Irish Assembly elections and 46.6% in the 2021 Senedd elections
In the 2024 Mayoral elections Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham was re-elected
with a turnout of just 32%, slightly lower than the previous election in 2021.
It can also be argued that the extent to which devolution has had a positive impact on
democracy has been limited by the fact that the UK government sometimes flouts the Sewel
convention, which holds that the UK Parliament shouldnʼt legislate on devolved areas for the
whole of the UK without a consent motion from the devolved bodies.
For example, Despite the law relating to public services, which are devolved, and the
Scottish and Welsh Parliaments withholding their consent, the UK Parliament passed the
Strikes Minimum Service Levels) Act 2023.
The devolved governments, particularly in Scotland and Wales, opposed the act,
viewing it as an infringement on their rights to manage public services and industrial
relations, whilst arguing that it undermines workers' rights.
The UK government proceeded with the legislation, citing the need for continuity of
critical public services, such as health, transport, and education, to protect public
welfare.
Parliamentary Sovereignty has been the bedrock of British democracy for centuries, yet
devolution undermines it by transferring power away from Parliament. It can therefore be
argued that this damages British democracy.

**************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, devolution has been a success in terms of democracy. Whilst it is true that devolution
does create policy differences for different UK citizens, this is ultimately a positive thing as it
reflects the different policy preferences of different parts of the UK and delivers local
representation.

Paragraph Two ➡ Impact Of Devolution On The Unity Of The UK
Against: Arguments That Devolution Hasnʼt Been A Success

The asymmetric nature of devolution, with different powers and policies for different parts of
the UK, can be seen as undermining equal citizenship, as different citizens have different levels
of representation, are subjected to different laws and to different policies.
This can be seen as threatening the unity of the UK, as it leads to its citizens having less in
common with each other.
For example, the Scottish and Northern Irish Parliaments control policing, whilst policing
in Wales and England is controlled by the UK Parliament.
The Barnett Formula, which allocates different amounts of funding per head to different
parts of the UK, with England in particular losing out, also has the potential to create
resentment between different parts of the UK.
It can be argued that devolution has fuelled increased nationalism and calls for independence,
by showing the ability of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland to govern themselves and giving
nationalist parties and politicians a platform.
The SNP has been in power in Scotland since 2007 and almost achieved independence in
2014. Since then, support among the Scottish population for independence has increased,
with most polls now suggesting there is majority support for independence.
Support for Welsh independence has also increased since devolution
In Northern Ireland, the more radical Sinn Fein has become the largest party in the
nationalist bloc and was the largest party following the 2022 Northern Irish Assembly
election.
Under recent Conservative governments, relations between devolved bodies/leaders and the
UK Government were very strained and fragmented, with significant tensions and a lack of
cooperation.
Boris Johnson failed to work closely with devolved administrations when in power, with
frequent clashes with the Scottish and Welsh governments over COVID policies and
constant criticism of Nicola Sturgeon in particular.
Mark Drakeford accused called Johnson “really, really awfulˮ for neglecting the Welsh
population in the way he formulated and announced COVID policies.
Rishi Sunak increased tensions hugely in 2023 by using a Section 35 Order for the first time
to block Scotlandʼs proposed Gender Reform Bill, which wouldʼve introduced self-
identification for those who wanted to change gender and allowed 16 and 17 year olds to do
so.
SNP leader Humza Yousaf unsuccessfully challenged this in the courts.
Further, in April 2025, it was clear that there are also tensions with the new Starmer
government, when Welsh FM Eluned Morgan said she will "call out" Labour in Westminster
when it "gets it wrong for Walesˮ.
She called for UK ministers to halt part of its plans to cut disability benefits, rethink cuts to
the winter fuel allowance, and demanded Wales benefit more from wind power via the
Crown Estate as Scotland does.

**************************************************************************************************

For: Arguments That Devolution Has Been A Success
One of the key aims of devolution was to keep the UK together. It was believed that granting
significant autonomy to Scotland within the United Kingdom in particular would prevent them
voting for independence.
As no nationalist movement has yet achieved independence and it could be argued that
devolution has therefore been a success in terms of the unity of the UK, as it has satisfied
some demands for self government and the UK hasnʼt broken up.
After the Troubles of the 70s, 80s and 90s, devolution has also played a key part in
delivering a relatively stable peace in Northern Ireland and provided a framework for long-
term change.
Rather than reflecting a problem with devolution itself, it can be argued that recent increases in
support for independence are a consequence of the unpopularity and failure of recent
Conservative governments.
Starmerʼs Labour government has so far taken a much more supportive and cooperative
approach to the devolved bodies than the previous Conservative governments under Sunak,
Truss and Johnson.
Starmer visited Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland within days of becoming Prime
Minister and held a meeting with all of Englandʼs regional mayors on his fifth day of office.
Labourʼs 2024 manifesto committed to establishing a new council of the nations and
regions, bringing together the leaders of all devolved governments and mayors.
Further, it committed to address and reduce breaches of the Sewel Convention by
setting out a new memorandum of understanding MoU, outlining how the nations will
work together for the common good.

**************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, devolution has certainly not been a success in terms of the unity of the UK. Since
devolution was introduced, there has been a marked increase in support for independence in
both Scotland and Wales, whilst recent years have seen major tensions between the UK
government and the leaders of devolved bodies.

Paragraph Three ➡ Impact Of Devolution On Governance
For: Arguments That Devolution Has Been A Success

It can be argued that devolution has been a success in terms of governance as in some areas
better results can be seen as a consequence of devolution.
A good example of devolution leading to better governance outcomes is in relation to
transport, which is controlled by almost all devolved bodies, including those in England.
In 2022, the Scottish government nationalised rail by bringing ScotRail under public
ownership. The Scottish government also subsidises rail fares, which leads to far
cheaper rail fares than in England.
For example, an off peak return from Glasgow to Edinburgh is around £10, whereas
a similar journey in England would likely be at least 3x the amount.
Andy Burnham has made a number of significant changes to local transport since
becoming mayor of Greater Manchester.
He has brought the bus service under local control, leading to fares being capped at
£2, even before they were in England.
He has also introduced the ‘Bee Networkʼ; a network of walking and cycling paths
aiming to connect every community in Greater Manchester.
Calls by devolved mayors for greater funding also led to local leaders being given more
than £1 billion to boost regional transport in March 2025, showing how the visibility that
devolved mayors bring to their regions can lead to increased focus, funding and policy
outcomes.
Devolution can also be argued to have a positive impact on the governance of the whole of the
UK, as policies which are successful in devolved bodies can be adopted in the rest of the UK,
such as the ban on smoking in public places which was first introduced in Scotland.
In Scotland and Wales, more proportional electoral systems havenʼt led to unstable coalition
governments. There have been mostly coalition/minority governments, but these have been
very stable and there have been a number of very popular leaders, particularly Nicola Sturgeon
and Mark Drakeford.

**************************************************************************************************

Against: Arguments That Devolution Hasnʼt Been A Success
On the other hand it can be argued that there has been little ‘devolution dividendʼ in terms of
economic and policy improvements, with devolved bodies even falling behind the rest of the UK
in some areas.
In August 2023, the Guardian reported that the devolved bodies had fallen behind England
in some key measures of NHS performance, despite them having significantly higher per
capita spending on health than England.
You are twice as likely to be on a year-plus waiting list in Scotland or Northern Ireland
than in England, whilst it is even worse in Wales, with 1 in 20 people on NHS waiting lists
for more than a year.
There are also significant differences in educational attainment between England and
the rest of the UK. Scotland and Wales have fallen behind England in the PISA ranking
(international rankings that measure educational performance).
The Scottish National Party SNP in particular has been criticised for focusing too much on
Scottish independence and neglecting day to day policymaking.
Further, the SNP has recently been rocked by a scandal in relation to its finances with
former chief executive of the SNP and husband of Nicola Sturgeon Peter Murrell arrested in
April 2023.
Whilst devolution may have led to stable governments in Scotland and Wales, this has certainly
not been the case in Northern Ireland. Government in Stormont is very fragile with frequent
suspensions in which the parties refuse to work together in government.
During these suspensions, government functions are decided directly by the government in
Westminster, during which some significant changes have been made, including the
legalisation of same sex marriage.
The Northern Ireland Assembly Stormont) was suspended for nearly 2 years following the
2022 Northern Ireland Assembly elections, as the DUP refused to go into coalition with Sinn
Fein in protest against the Northern Ireland Protocol.
This shows how the structure of Northern Irish Devolution, which requires power
sharing, often leads to failure in governance and control having to be still exerted by the
UK Parliament.

**************************************************************************************************

Judgement

Overall, devolution can be seen as relative success in terms of governance in Wales and
Scotland, which have had stable and relatively popular governments, but a failure in Northern
Ireland, where the government is frequently suspended.

**************************************************************************************************

Overall Argument
In conclusion, devolution has been a success according to some of its aims but a failure in
others.
It can be seen as a success in terms of delivering greater democracy and local representation,
promoting effective governance in Scotland and Wales and maintaining peace in Northern
Ireland.
It should be seen as a failure, however, in terms of maintaining the unity of the UK and providing
a framework for effective governance in Northern Ireland.
