### Timeline

- 1945 - End of Second World War - **unusual willingness to think in European rather than national terms**, to sort the wreckage of the war. Monnet and others wish for an economic, political and military union on supranational terms. ^68c423
- 1950 - Schuman Declaration: set up a supranational body to **manage coal and steel production in Germany and France** (and any others who were interested), forming a tariff-free market. This became the European Coal and Steel Community, with Germany, France, Italy and the Benelux countries as members. Also set up Euratom. 
- 1957 (March) - Treaty of Rome establishes European Economic Community (also known as the Common Market), **aiming to remove internal trade barriers,** harmonious economic activities, balanced expansion, increased stability, accelerated improvements in living standards and closer relations between states. 
- 1967 - **ECSC, Euratom (control of nuclear weapons in Europe) and EEC merge to form the European Community.**
- 1973 - Britain, Denmark and Ireland join. British voters confirm their membership in the **1975 European Communities referendum.**
- 1979 - European Parliament (weak) directly elected for the first time.
- **1986 - Member states (inc. UK) sign Single European Act, creating the single market, with free movement of goods, persons, services and capital. Portugal and Spain join.** - [[four-freedoms-(EU)]]
- **1993 - Maastricht Treaty signed, creating the European Union and planning for a Single Currency, the Social Chapter, and EU citizenship.**
- 1995 - Austria, Finland and Sweden join. Also, *the Schengen Agreement* takes effect in seven countries-Belgium, Germany, Spain, France, Luxembourg, the Netherlands and Portugal. Travellers of any nationality can travel between all these countries without any passport control. Others have since joined the Schengen zone. 
- 1997 - Treaty of Amsterdam signed, laying down plans to reform EU institutions, to give Europe a stronger voice in the world.
- 2001 - Treaty of Nice: focussed on enlargement of the EU, extending the Parliament's co-decision powers and reworking to QMV system on the Council of Ministers.
- 2002 The single currency: Euro notes and coins arrive.
- 2004 - EU Constitution: sought to bring together all existing treaties into one codified document. It would in effect have strengthened the European Parliament, but was rejected by France and the Netherlands in referendums in 2005. Also, Cyprus, Czech Republic, Estonia, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Malta, Poland, Slovakia and Slovenia join.
- 2007 - Lisbon Treaty: Prominent changes included the move from required unanimity to double majority voting in several policy areas in the Council of Ministers, a more powerful European Parliament, and the creation of a long-term President of the European Council and a High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy. The Treaty also made the Union's bill of rights, the Charter of Fundamental Rights, legally binding. Also, Bulgaria and Romania join.
- 2016 - UK voters decide to leave the European Union in the EU Referendum (Leave 51.8% vs Remain 48.11%). Article 50 of the Lisbon Treaty (the official mechanism for leaving the EU) was officially triggered by Theresa May's Conservative government in March 2017, giving the UK 2 years to leave the EU. The UK eventually exits in 2020.
