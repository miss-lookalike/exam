Supreme Court :: The highest court in the UK political system Established in 2009, it has 12 judges who carry out judgements of great legal, constitutional and politics significance.

Judicial Neutrality :: The principle that judges should not be influenced by their personal political opinions and should remain outside of party politics, so they can apply the law impartially to all.

Judicial Independence :: The principle that judges should not be influenced by other branches of government, particularly the [[executive]]. This is essential if judges are to apply the law fairly to everyone.

Judicial Review :: The power of the judiciary to review, and sometimes reverse, actions by other branches of government that breach the law or that are incompatible with the Human Rights Act.

Elective dictatorship :: A government that dominates Parliament, usually due to a large majority, and therefore has few limits on its power.

Lord Hailsham coined the term 'Elective dictatorship' in 1976 which is:

A system of government where the [[executive]] dominates the legislature. The leader is democratically elected, btu the power and authority of [[Parliament]] is concentrated in the hands of any party leader who can win and control a large majority in the [[house-of-commons]].

EU :: A political and economic union of a group of European countries. Established in 1957 as the European Economic Community; renamed after the Maastricht Treaty in 1993.

Four Freedoms :: The principle of free movement of goods, services, capital and people within the EU's single market.

Legal Sovereignty :: The legal right to exercise [[sovereignty]] - i.e. sovereignty in practice, which ultimately derives from the public in democracy

Ultra Vires :: Literally ''beyond the powers'. An action that is taken without legal authority when it requires it, but the [[supreme-court]] can also quash [[executive]] actions which have been taken ultra vires, because they are unlawful.
