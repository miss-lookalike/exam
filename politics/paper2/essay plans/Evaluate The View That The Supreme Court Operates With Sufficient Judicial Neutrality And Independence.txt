Evaluate The View That The Supreme Court Operates With Sufficient Judicial Neutrality And Independence. 

Introduction
Key Definitions
Judicial neutrality is the idea that judges will exercise their functions without being influenced
by their own personal bias or political opinions.
Judicial independence is the idea that judges must be free from political interference,
particularly from the government.

**************************************************************************************************
**************************************************************************************************

Paragraph One ➡ How The Judicial Neutrality Of The Supreme
Court Is Protected
For: The Supreme Court Does Operate With Sufficient Judicial Neutrality
There are a number of different rules that Supreme Court justices have to follow to ensure they
remain neutral when exercising the law, many of these were included in the 2005 Constitutional
Reform Act.
Judges, like civil servants, aren't allowed to campaign for political parties or pressure groups.
Judges have to base each decision in law and provide a full explanation of how they reached it.
Supreme Court decisions are published in full for people to read and scrutinise, whilst cases are
televised on YouTube.
This attempts to ensure that the Supreme Court is transparent and that it fulfils its role
without being influenced by personal political views.
To get onto the Supreme Court, a judge has to have served as a senior judge for 2 years, or
been a qualified lawyer for at least 15 years and their elevation to the Supreme Court is
based on their past performance and experience, in which they have been neutral.
The current President of the Supreme Court Lord Robert Reed, for example, has served
as a senior judge since 1998.
Finally, conflicts of interest are avoided by preventing Supreme Court judges from sitting on
cases that involve family, friends and others they know personally.

**************************************************************************************************

Against: The Supreme Court Doesnʼt Operate With Sufficient Judicial Neutrality
One key way in which the neutrality of the Supreme Court has been questioned is in relation to
its very narrow composition in terms of gender, age, education, class and race. It is argued that
the courtʼs narrow composition leads to limited perspectives and potentially to an inability to
fully understand certain issues/the situations of certain individuals.
Of the current 12 justices: 10 are male, 11 are white, 11 have studied at Oxbridge, 12 are 60 or
over and the majority went to private schools.
This led to the Times describing the Supreme Court in 2011 as ‘pale, male and staleʼ.
The fact that the court only had one female member was significant in the case of 'Radmacher
v Granatino' 2010. It was a case involving a prenuptial agreement between marriage partners,
in which a majority of the justices upheld the principle that claims made in the event of a
divorce should be limited.
Lady Hale, the only female justice, was the only one of the 9 justices to dissent from the
majority verdict.
She gave as her reason that the vast majority of people who would lose out as a result of
the precedent would be women and argued that it may be better for Parliament to make
such decisions where gender is a key element of the case, rather than a very
unrepresentative Supreme Court.
She therefore argued that the unrepresentative makeup of the Supreme Court impeded its
ability to be neutral.
This is especially worrying if one takes the perspective that the power of the Supreme Court to
interpret the law and judge whether individuals and public bodies have broken it is a quasi-
legislative function that grants them a lot of power.
COUNTERPOINT On the other hand, it could be argued that irrespective of the Supreme Courtʼs
composition, they are still neutral as they base very decision in law and simply implement the
law.

**************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, the more persuasive argument is that the very narrow composition of the Supreme
Court impedes its ability to be act with judicial neutrality, as it limited perspectives and the
enforcement of the law through the perspective of old, well educated white men.\

Paragraph Two ➡ How The Judicial Independence Of The
Supreme Court Is Protected
For: The Supreme Court Does Operate With Sufficient Judicial Independence
Prior to the Constitutional Reform Act, there were a lot of concerns about how independent the
Supreme Court was, largely due to the fact that the Law Lords were within the legislature and
the Lord Chancellor appointed senior judges whilst being a government minister and chairing
sitting in the House of Lords.
The creation of the Supreme Court changed this and removed the legislative and judicial
role of the Lord Chancellor, who is now just a government minister.
The House of Lords is now chaired by the Lord Speaker and Supreme Court justices are
now appointed by an independent 5-member Judicial Appointments Commission and
the judiciary is headed by the most senior member of the Supreme Court, who is known
as the ‘Lord Chief Justiceʼ.
This can be seen as ensuring the Supreme Court operates with sufficient judicial
independence, as the government now doesnʼt directly select the highest judges in the
country.
There are a number of other ways in which the judicial independence of the Supreme Court is
guaranteed, including security of tenure, protection of pay and sub judice rules.
Judges can't be removed from office unless they break the law or are impeached by a vote
in both houses of parliament. The only limit on their service is an official retirement age of
70, which is known as a security of tenure.
This ensures they are independent by preventing the government from removing them
and ensuring they can carry out justice without the fear of being sacked.
Judges' salaries are paid automatically from an independent budget known as the
consolidation funds, which canʼt be manipulated by the government and they are paid well
so that there is less incentive for them to be influenced by financial pressure from political
actors.
The current salary of Supreme Court justices is £226,193.
Sub judice rules prevent members of parliament, government ministers and the media from
publicly speaking about impending or ongoing legal proceedings.
This is intended to prevent the media and politicians from influencing the outcome of
court cases.

**************************************************************************************************

Against: The Supreme Court Doesnʼt Operate With Sufficient Judicial
Independence
On the other hand, it can be argued that the protection of judicial independence has been
weakened recently, due to the increased media scrutiny of the judiciary and the Supreme Court,
particularly in relation to Brexit and immigration.
Especially surrounding two key cases in relation to Brexit (the Article 50 case and the
Prorogation case), the work of the Supreme Court has become increasingly high profile and
politicised in recent years.
Supreme Court justices are no longer as anonymous as they used to be, whilst various parts
of the media and politicians, including government ministers, have commented on and
criticised the decisions of the court.
This can be seen as putting undue pressure on the Supreme Court and threatening its
ability to make independent decisions.
The Supreme Courtʼs ruling that the Rwanda plan was illegal under domestic and international
law led to criticism from key members of the Conservative Party.
Sunak criticised the ruling in the media, with his rhetoric presenting the court as interfering
in policy, with the implication of judicial overreach.
Even though he knows full well the court isnʼt a foreign court, he said in the wake of the
ruling “Iʼve also been clear that I wonʼt allow a foreign court to block us from flights
taking off. My patience is worn thin, the British peopleʼs patience is worn thin.ˮ
There is significant concern that ministers attacking judges over their decisions and
criticising the courts puts undue pressure on the court, flouting sub judice rules and making
it more difficult for the court to act neutrally.
Prime Minister Starmer was criticised by by Lady Chief Justice Carr (head of the judiciary but
not on the Supreme Court) in early 2025 after he stated that a judicial decision regarding the
resettlement of a Gazan family in the UK was "completely wrongˮ and that a legal loophole
would be closed as a result.
The Lady Chief Justice said that the Government failed to visibly respect and protect the
independence of the judiciary, and any dissent should be made through the appellate
process not verbal attacks, in order to protect judicial independence.
This shows how the government's criticism of judges threatens the independence of the
judiciary.

**************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, despite the increased media scrutiny of the Supreme Court in recent years, it arguably
still operates with sufficient judicial independence, particularly due to the reforms to the
appointment process introduced by the Constitutional Reform Act 2005.

Paragraph Three ➡ Recent Brexit-Focused Court Cases
Against For: The Supreme Court Doesnʼt Operate With Sufficient Judicial
Independence and Neutrality
The neutrality of key courts, including the Supreme Court, has been questioned in relation to
important court cases that relate to Brexit, particularly the Article 50 and Prorogation cases.
After the High Court ruled in November 2016 that the consent of Parliament was needed to
trigger Article 50, which officially started the process of Brexit, the Daily Mail ran the
headline ‘Enemies Of The Peopleʼ with pictures of the 3 high court judges, suggesting that
they were defying democracy and the wishes of 17.4M Brexit voters.
After the Supreme Court ruled in 2019 that Boris Johnsonʼs prorogation of Parliament was
unlawful, various right wing commentators and Johnson allies described the Supreme Court
judges as ‘arch-remainersʼ.
Critics therefore argue that the Supreme Court was not acting with sufficient judicial neutrality
and was influenced by anti-Brexit views that were common among those with high levels of
education.
Some also argued that the Article 50 and Prorogation cases were examples of the Supreme
Court seeking to interfere too much in politics and limit the actions of a democratically
elected government.

**************************************************************************************************

For: The Supreme Court Does Operate With Sufficient Judicial Independence
and Neutrality
On the other hand, it can be argued that the Supreme Court was operating with sufficient
judicial neutrality and independence in these cases. Even though they may have made the
Brexit process more difficult, they were simply upholding the constitution.
Rather than the Supreme Court seeking to interfere in political and stop the wishes of a
democratically elected government, they were simply upholding the law against a
government that was seeking to act ‘ultra viresʼ.
In the Article 50 case, they were right to require Parliamentʼs consent as triggering Article 50
and starting the process of leaving the EU involved the removal of rights conferred by
parliament (passing the 1972 European Communities Act.
In the prorogation case too, the Supreme Court simply prevented Boris Johnson from avoiding
parliamentary scrutiny through using the power to prorogue for the governmentʼs benefit,
upholding the sovereignty of Parliament.

**************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, the more persuasive argument is that the Supreme Court did operate with sufficient
judicial neutrality and independence in the Article 50 and Prorogation court cases.
The court was simply upholding the law and defending the sovereignty of Parliament against
governments that sought to act ultra vires.

Overall Argument
In conclusion, the Supreme Court does operate with sufficient judicial independence, but not
with sufficient judicial neutrality. The reforms made to the appointments process in the
Constitutional Reform Act in particular play a key role in ensuring the Supreme Court is
independent from any political pressure, as seen in the Article 50 and Prorogation court cases.
The very narrow composition of the court in terms of gender, race and educational background
in particular limit the extent to which it can be said to be neutral in ever case, however. To make
the court truly neutral, there needs to be greater efforts to make the court representative of the
population.
