Exclusive Competencies - Only the EU makes policy, and national governments have no say or role. Applies to customs, external trade, monetary policy in the Eurozone, and marine conservation.

Shared competencies - [[european-union-(EU)|EU]] has priority, but if it chooses not to make policy in these areas, member states can act as they see fit. Applies to social policy, employment, territorial cohesion, environment, transport, energy, security, justice, freedom, and agriculture and fisheries.

Supporting competencies - The [[european-union-(EU)|EU]] may coordinate or support the actions of members states, but cannot make policy decisions itself. Applies to culture, industry, education, healthcare and foreign and security policy

Exclusive member state competencies - The [[european-union-(EU)|EU]] can play no role in these areas. Applies to the level of income tax within member states, or public spending on social services.
