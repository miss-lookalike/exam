
**(R) Miller vs Secretary of State for Exiting the EU (2017)**
When the Brexit referendum led the government to claim it could initiate the legal process of leaving the EU, the Supreme Court voted by 8–3 to judge that the government did not have the authority to do this. Parliament had legislated to take the UK into the EC in 1972, so Parliament must authorise any decision to remove the UK from the EU.
**Influence:** Big influence

**Joanna Cherry / Gina Miller case (2019)**
Gina Miller and SNP MP Joanna Cherry brought cases against PM Boris Johnson's prorogation of Parliament, arguing that the PM was intentionally limiting Parliament's ability to debate and oppose his EU withdrawal proposals. Both cases were referred to the Supreme Court, which unanimously found that the PM's prorogation was illegal due to "preventing the ability of Parliament to carry out its constitutional functions" (Lady Hale).
**Influence:** Big influence

**HJ and HT vs Home Secretary (2010)**
The Home Office refused to grant asylum to two men from Iran and Cameroon who claimed asylum because they were gay and would suffer persecution in their home countries. The Home Office argued the men could avoid persecution by concealing their sexuality, but the Supreme Court decided against this, judging that requiring the men to conceal their sexuality infringed their human rights.
**Influence:** Yes

---

**R (on the application of Unison) v Lord Chancellor (2017)**
Trade union UNISON argued that the government’s introduction of fees for employment tribunals (which make judgments in cases like unfair dismissal) was unlawful. The Supreme Court agreed, ruling against the government, judging that the fees were unlawful because they had the effect of denying justice to those on lower incomes.
**Influence:** Significant

---

**AM (Zimbabwe) v Secretary of State for the Home Department (2020)**
The Supreme Court blocked attempts by the Home Office to deport an individual convicted of several serious offences on the basis that the individual’s life would likely be shortened by being sent back to Zimbabwe, because he would not get the necessary treatment for his HIV. Here, the Court needed to make a judgement on individual vs collective rights.
**Influence:** Significant

---

**Shamima Begum (2021)**
Having left the UK in 2015 to join Islamic State in Syria, Shamima Begum’s citizenship was removed by then Home Secretary Sajid Javid in 2019 on the basis that she was a threat to national security. In 2020, the Court of Appeal ruled that she should be allowed to travel to the UK to appeal the judgement, but the Supreme Court overruled this, judging in favour of the government’s view that Begum’s right to a fair trial did not trump the requirements of national security.
**Influence:** Not significant
