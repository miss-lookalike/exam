
**Factortame (1991)**
The High Court ruled that the UK Merchant Fishing Act was in conflict with the EU Fisheries Directives. The UK law was set aside, establishing the principle that EU law supersedes UK law.
**Influence:** Incredibly significant – deemed EU law higher

---

**Belmarsh Case (2004)**
The House of Lords ruled that detainees held against their will under anti-terrorism legislation contradicted the Human Rights Act on the grounds that citizens have the right not to be detained without trial.
**Influence:** Hugely significant – doesn't mean the prisoners are released

---

**Suspected Terrorist Bank Assets (2010)**
The Supreme Court ruled that the government did not have the legal power to freeze the assets of suspected terrorists. However, the government then used Parliament to pass special legislation to give them this power, so Parliament ultimately prevailed over the Court.
**Influence:** Not very significant – parliamentary sovereignty won

---

**The Status of the Human Rights Act (1998)**
The Court can declare bills or Acts of Parliament incompatible with the Human Rights Act (which is aligned with the European Convention on Human Rights), pressuring Parliament to reconsider. Parliament does not have to comply but often does. Since 2015, the Conservative Party has debated replacing the HRA with a British Bill of Rights, which it could do with a simple majority in Parliament because the HRA is just ordinary statute law rather than a "higher law."
**Influence:** Not that significant

---

**R (Steinfield and Keiden) vs Secretary of State for International Development**
The Court declared that the Civil Partnership Act 2004 was incompatible with the ECHR because it precluded a different-sex couple from entering into a civil partnership. This led to Parliament amending the legislation by passing the Civil Partnerships, Marriages and Deaths (Registration etc) Act 2019.
**Influence:** \[Not stated, but implied to be moderate/significant]

---

**P v Cheshire West and Cheshire Council (2014)**
In the case of ‘P’ (a man with Down’s syndrome and cerebral palsy) who was placed in care with limitations on his personal freedom, the Supreme Court played a significant role in determining the meaning of liberty. Lady Hale stated that disability should not deny people their human rights to freedom even if they were receiving good quality care: “a gilded cage is still a cage.”
**Influence:** \[Not stated, but implied to be significant]
