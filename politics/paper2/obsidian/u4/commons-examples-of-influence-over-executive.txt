### Examples of weak parliamentary influence 

- 1979-87 - Margaret Thatcher's determined leadership of the [[Conservative party]] contrasted with divisions within labour which led to the party fracturing and the foundation of the social democratic party in 1981. In 1983 she gained a parliamentary majority of 144 seats, this was partly due to the Falklands war the year before. Neither Michael Foot (1980-83) or Neil Kinnock (1983-92) was able to effectively challenge.
- 1997-2001 - Tony Blair won a landslide election with a majority of 179 seats. Labour united around [[Third Way]] policies and the conservatives had the worst result since 1832 with 165 [[MP]]s. They were further undermined by divisions over EU and poor leadership by William Hague (1997-2001)

### Examples of strong parliamentary influence

- 1974-79 (Wilson and Callaghan) - although Harold Wilson won the October 1974 election he secured a majority of just 3. James Callaghan took over in 1976, Labour's majority disappeared and Labour entered a confidence and supply agreement with the Liberal party 1977-78. After this the government struggled until losing a vote of confidence 310-311 and Thatcher won the election.
- 2017-19 - Theresa May decided to call a snap election to try increase her majority but instead found herself as a minority leader. She then had to enter a confidence and supply agreement with the DUP. This made it harder to pass Brexit policies which was only worsened by Conservative divisions and growing Labour confidence. Massive defeats of her Brexit legislation in January and March shifted power towards parliament.
- 2019 (Johnson) - His Brexit proposals were even more unpopular with pro-European Conservatives and he suffered the humiliation of Parliament seizing control of parliamentary business and enacting the Benn Bill (4 September 2019) extending the Brexit deadline if a deal was not achieved.

However, not even a powerful government should take Parliament for granted. In 1986, despite its 144-seat majority, the Thatcher government was defeated on the second reading of the Shops Bill, which would have enabled shops to open on Sunday, when 72 Conservative MPs voted against the bill. In 2021, 99 Conservative MPs voted against Covid passes, forcing Boris Johnson to rely on Labour support for the bill to pass the House of Commons. In 2022, Johnson was forced to resign as prime minister when he lost the support of both his cabinet and the parliamentary Conservative Party as a result of illegal Downing Street parties during lockdown.

### Focus - Parliamentary debate

The key reason Cameron did not take action in Syria in 2013 was because he lost the debate. The measure was put forward to use military strikes on the Syrian government due to allegations of chemical weapons being used. The government was defeated by 285-272. Thirty Conservative and nine Liberal Democrat [[MP]]s voted against the government.
