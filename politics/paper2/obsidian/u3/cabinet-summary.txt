---

- 20-25 members, all governing party members 
- All appointed (and dismissed) by the [[prime-minister|PM]] personally, with no need to consult anyone else 
- Most are heads of large [[government-department|government departments]]; a few are not, but are important party members 
- All are MPs or Peers (mostly MPs) - from [[parliament]]
- Meetings chaired + agenda set by the PM 
- [[Cabinet]] Secretary (civil servant) takes the minutes (a record of the meetings), which the PM approves 
- Meets once a week on a Thursday for around 2 hours, though emergency meetings can be called

---

## Questions

- Why are cabinet members all governing party members who are [[MP]]s or [[peers]]?
	- Gives them democratic authority
- Why are there around 20-25 of them?
	- There's 19 departments + a few extras
- How much control does the [[prime-minister]] have over proceedings?
	- Almost all of it

---

- **Main role is to legitimise decisions** made by the PM and their special advisers - this approval turns ideas into 'Official Policy', but it is often just a formality ('rubber-stamping') with no real debate 
- **There are no votes**: the PM asks all members to agree to policies, and if they disagree strongly enough they must resign and leave the Cabinet 
- **Proceedings are secret for 30 years**, but can be released by the PM to a strictly limited number of civil servants. Media releases can be sent out, but with no real details of discussions.
- Cabinet members get an enhanced salary
