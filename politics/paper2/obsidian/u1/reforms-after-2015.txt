**1. Reform: Local government act**
**Date and description:**
Metro-Mayors: Continued introduction of elected mayors in some areas, including some without referendums (e.g. West Midlands, West of England, Liverpool City Region; by 2022 the number had risen to 9, including Sheffield City Region, North of Tyne, and West Yorkshire - attempting to balance the sense the North of England is underrepresented in Parliament). These metro-mayors have significant powers and budgets.
In 2016, the Greater Manchester elected mayor became responsible for a £6 billion health and social care budget.
However, by 2022 the electorate had rejected the opportunity to have an elected mayor in 37 out of 54 referendums that were called; in May 2022, Bristol voted to abolish its elected mayoral position on a turnout of 29%.
**Significance score:** 7/10

**2. Reform: Devolution**
**Date and description:**

* **2016:** Extension of Scottish Devolution (Scotland Act 2016) following the independence referendum in 2014. The referendum result was that Scotland should remain as part of the UK, but the 45% who voted to leave the UK were appeased by further measures of devolution of power to Scotland, giving Scotland a great deal of independence over financial matters (known as ‘Devo-Max’).
* **2017:** The Wales Act 2017 gave further administrative and political powers to Wales, as well as requiring a referendum before any future abolition of the Welsh National Assembly, weakening Westminster’s parliamentary sovereignty. Quasi-Federal.
  **Significance score:** 8/10

**3. Reform: Fixed-term Parliaments Act**
**Date and description:**
2011: Improved democracy by removing the Prime Minister’s power to choose when general elections took place, instead insisting that they happen every 5 years except under exceptional circumstances (i.e. if the government loses a vote of confidence, or if the House of Commons votes by a two-thirds majority to call an election).
The impact of the act was weakened when Theresa May used the two-thirds vote provision to call a snap election in 2017, whilst Boris Johnson did the same in 2019 with the Conservatives' ‘Early Parliamentary Act’.
As a result of these weaknesses, Parliament (using its parliamentary sovereignty) repealed this act in 2022.
**Significance score:** 2/10

**4. Reform: English Votes for English Laws**
**Date and description:**
2011: To address the West Lothian Question, a change in parliamentary procedure meant MPs from Scottish constituencies could no longer vote to impose policies that only applied to England (e.g. education).
However, this procedure was only in force from October 2015 until July 2021, when it was removed altogether following a decision of the House.
**Significance score:** 2/10

**5. Reform: Recall of MPs**
**Date and description:**
2015: Improved accountability by allowing MPs who had been imprisoned or suspended from the House of Commons to be re-called (i.e. a by-election would be triggered) if 10% of their constituents signed a petition.
**Significance score:** 6/10
