They embrace meritocracy, equality of welfare and equality of opportunity.&nbsp;</li></ul>

How do socialists agree and disagree on the economy?	"<ul><li>All socialists see a productive economy as positive – the only problem is how the wealth is shared.</li><li>Fundamentalist socialists wish to abolish the capitalist economic system so that
the wealth created by the economy can be equally owned and distributed in common rather than in private hands.&nbsp;</li><li>Revisionist socialists are less hostile towards capitalism and believe it can be reformed and harnessed to work for the good of all in society</li><ul>
</ul>


</ul>"

How do socialists differ on how to 'achieve' socialism?	<ul><li>Social democrats have opted for a 'gradual road', operating within the confines of democracy.&nbsp;</li><li>Maxists believe the capitalist system must be destroyed through revolution to achieve socialism.&nbsp;</li><li>Evolutionary socialists like Beatrice Webb believe that socialism can be achieved through Parliamentary democracy</li></ul>
What are the three assumptions of social democrats?	"<div>
<div>
<div>
<div>
<ul>
<li>

</div>

What are the 5 main features of third way thinking?	<ol><li><b>The primary of the market and rejection of state intervention -&nbsp;</b>Dynamic market economy to foster growth and wealth creation, in a globalisaed economy.&nbsp;</li><li><b>The value of community and moral responsibility</b> - Personal autonomy operates within a communal context based on mutual dependence and benefit, balancing rights with responsibilities.&nbsp;</li><li>A <b>social model based on harmony and consensus</b>. - not class conflict/inequality.&nbsp;</li><li>Social inclusion - equality of opportunity and <b>meritoracy</b>. Welfare should allow people to improve their opportunities.&nbsp;</li><li><b>An entrepreurial state</b> that develops the skills of the workforce through investment.&nbsp;</li></ol>
