Collectivism 1   Main idea ___ socialism -> also informing ___ What is the can be achieved through banding together? Is society fixed?   Endorsements of collectivism: (moral/economic perspectives) What socialist view does this reflect?	- Main idea underpinning socialism -> also informing equality, welfare and common ownership - Maintains that humans can achieve their social, economic and political objectives more effectively by banding together through collective effort - Also implies that society can be transformed through collective endeavour -> can reach ideal society   - Endorse collectivism because: 1. moral perspective -> interests of the group should weigh more than self-interest, collectivism encourages social responsibility 2. in economic terms -> collectivism utilises the capabilities of the whole society which is seen as more efficient - Reflects the socialist view that the collective is more important than individual self-interest
Collectivism 2 / human nature    Collectivism rooted in human nature:   Humans are what type of animal? Humans have capacity for what? Tied by what kind of bonds How is human nature molded and how can true freedom be achieved	Emphasis on collectivism rooted in socialist view of human nature:    -> argues that humans are social animals who prefer to live within society than alone -> humans have capacity for collective action and can work together in order to achieve their goals -> Tied by bonds of fraternity  -> also argue that human nature is moulded by social conditions and experiences of life which leads socialists to conclude that membership to a community is the gateway to true freedom and fufilment
Definition - fraternity	Literally a 'brotherhood', humans are bound together by comradeship and a common outlook due to sharing the same basic nature and interests
Collectivism 3 - difference of opinions   - Most socialists call for ___   - 2 differing strands with examples	- Most socialists call for some form of state intervention and state planning to promote collectivist goals and ensure there is an equitable distribution of g & s  - However extent to commitment to collectivism dependant on strand:   Marxists: - Advocate collective action through a centralised state which organises almost all production and distribution of resources  - eg Russian agriculture under Stalin in 1930s   Moderate socialists: - Accept some degree of free-market capitalism as necessary and have pursued collective goals in a more limited way - eg 1945-51 Labour nationalisation of key industries like coal, iron and steel

Equality 2 - Debates about how equality should be achieved and what it looks like   Ops of x; - Demand ___ - Such equality can only come through ____ - Therefore reject what   y: - Call for more limited ___ to achieve ____ - Primary aim ____ - State should ___ - What is necessary?	- Key debate around how social and economic equality can or should achieved (mainly about role of state)    - Revolutionary socialsists, Marxists, demand absolute equality for everyone in terms of material rewards and opportunities -> such equality can only come about through the controlled distribution of g & s, common ownership and abolition of private property -> therefore reject capitalism and the free market and claim revolution needed   - Social democrats call for more limited state intervention to achieve relative equality in society via welfare measures and progressive taxation - Primary aim to remove absolute poverty, some inequality would then be tolerated - State should not own the factors of production instead should just aim to minimise the wealth divide - Material incentives seen as necessary for functioning economy
Common ownership 1   3 drawbacks of private property: ?	Drawbacks of private property: 1. As wealth is created by communal effort it should be owned collectively, not by a few 2. Private property encourages materialism and fosters the false belief that wealth brings self-fufilment 3. Private property generates social conflict between upper and lower classes
Common ownership 2   What do socialists believe should be abolished and to what extents  USSR: More moderate socialists have opted for _____	- Due to the drawbacks of private property socialists come to the conclusion that it should be abolished and replaced by common ownership however the severity of such abolishment is under debate - USSR 1930s Stalin's regime implemented almost complete common ownership through the forced nationalisation of most key industries - More moderate socialists have opted for more limited common ownership through the nationalisation of key industries eg Labour under Attlee 1945-51 nationalising key industries like coal, steel and railways
Disagreements among socialists - Absolute equality    What is it? Supported by who on the basis of ___ Rejected by who on the basis of ___	- Absolute equality is based on the notion that everyone will recieve the same rewards, providing they make a contribution to society, over time each person will make broadly equal contributions   -> approach supported by Marxists as the basis of a communist society -> rejected by the social democrats and the Third way as impractical and destabilising
Disagreements among socialists - Equality of opportunity    Description '____' Supported by ___ Rejected by ___	- Equality of opportunity is based on the principle that everyone should have equal chance to make the best of their abilities - 'Level playing field' with no artificial barriers to progress - Supported by social democrats and Third Way on grounds of fairness - Rejected by Marxists as seen as an acceptence of capitalism and its structural inequalities rather than seeking its abolishment
Disagreements among socialists - Equality of welfare   Description Endorsed by who because Rejected by who because	-> Equality of welfare accepts that human society is inevitably unequal but also maintains that every individual is entitled to have an equal minimum standard of living guaranteed by state welfare   - Endorsed by social democrats and Third Way as it provides vital safety net for most vunerable  - Marxists reject because the provision of welfare does not seek to abolish capitalism and its structural inequalities

What do the different factions aim for?	- For socialists, existance of social class highlights the divide in society  - Socialists have used social class to reach conclusion that people with a similar socio-economic position in society share a similair outlook and have common aims -> linked to endorsement of collectivism - Socialism's focus on social class is based on ideological commitment to change conditions of working class - Working class represents the means for which a socialist transformation of society can come about  - Communists aim for a classless society whilst modern socialists tend to aim to reduce class differences
Social class 2 - Disagreements   Marxist views - How is a persons class determined - Difference between bourgeoisie and proletariat - What is inevitable and when will state wither   SD views - What do they accept - Emphasise the need to ___ through ___ -> see the state as ___	- Disagreements over importance   - Marxists tend to emphasise the fundamental role of class politics -> in their analysis a person's class position is determined by their relationship to the means of production  -> those who own the means are the bourgeoisie and those selling their labour are proletariat -> due to the exploitation of the proletariat by the bourgois state Marxist theory predicts an inevitable revolution will overthrow the bourgeoisie due to this class conflict -> the state will only wither away when the social class differences are replaced by a classless society    - Social democrats define social class in more fluid and less extreme terms -> accept there will always be some differences in class -> emphasise the need to narrow (not remove) class differences through targeted state intervention  -> see state as a tool to provide welfare and redistribute rather than an instrument of oppressive class rule

C.S  Russian Revolution	- In 1917 the Russian economy collapsed under the strain of WW1 - Workers' factory committees were established to superwise or replace managers and try and maintain production - Within months these committees controled roughly 40% of the industrial working class - Bolsheviks issued additional powers to the factory committees later that year - Factory committees later merged with trade unions under firm Bolshevik control
C.S Syndicalism in France   What was the movement and when? How was the order to be destroyed? what would future society look like?	- Militant trade-union movement began in France in 1890s heavily influenced by George Sorel's thinking on direct action and the use of general strike to achieve working class objectives - Once a general strike had destroyed capitalist order, syndicalists envisaged a system where each industry was run by trade unions and the state would be replaced by a federation of trade union bodies providing full control to the workers'
