What is atomism?	<ul><li>The idea that society is made up of self-interested and self sufficient individuals (also known as egotistical individualism)</li></ul>
Why are humans psychologically imperfect?&nbsp;	<ul><li>Because humans are limited and depdent. Crave safety, familiarity and the security of knowing their designated place in society.&nbsp;</li><li>Hence why social order is more important than liberty (Hobbes, Leviathan). Social order gives the stability</li></ul>
Why are humans morally imperfect?	<ul><li>Naturally selfish and greedy.&nbsp;</li><ul><li>Can be backed up by Hobbes and Oakeshott</li></ul><li>Anti-social or criminal behaviour is due to basic human nature&nbsp;</li><li>A robust law system that imposes sanctions on conduct is the only effective deterrend to combat the moral imperfections of humans</li></ul>
Why are humans intellectually imperfect?	<ul><li>Because reasoning of humans is limited. Do not possess the mental faculties to make sense of a complex and modern world</li><li>Hence why Conservatives reject overarching ideologies or theories that explain or predict the devopment of human society.&nbsp;</li><li>Instead, humans need to draw on tradition and history to understand their place in the world</li></ul>
Why do Conservatives believe in an organic society?	<ul><li>Because, individuals are security and dependency seeking -&nbsp;</li><li><b>society provides individuals with a sense of security and purpose</b>. <b>Prevents the breakdown of social standards&nbsp;</b></li><li>Society is like an organism, with all parts working to maintain harmony</li></ul>
Why can the internal elements of an organic society or state not be randomly reconfigured?	<ul><li>Because like a living creature, an organic society is maintained by a delicate set of relationships.</li><li>If the balance is undermined by rapid upheval, society will be undermined</li></ul>
Why is an organic society necessary to satisfy human nature?	<ul><li>Natural needs such as affection, security, concern, rather than ideological bueprint devised by political theorists.&nbsp;</li><li>Component parts of society have been moulded by natural forces beyond human control - members should maintain this careful balance of interacting elements.&nbsp;</li><li>Long standing institutions have played a key role in preserving the 'health' of society and should not be changed or removed</li></ul>
Why is an organic society based on&nbsp;<b>hierarchy?</b>	<ul><li>&nbsp;Individuals vary in terms of talent, skills and work rate.&nbsp;</li><li>Organic society must rest on inequality because <b>different classes and groups (like different limbs and organs) have to perform specific roles</b> - mangagers, bureaucrats.&nbsp;</li><ul><li>This is justifiable because the most advantaged also bear the heaviest social responsibility - noblesse oblige. Have to protect workers. Encourages paternalism</li></ul></ul>
Why is authority important to conservatives?	<ul><li>Hierarchical structure due to differences in status enforces authority.&nbsp;</li><li><b>Authority provides humans with security, direction and support.&nbsp;</b></li><li><b>Offers discipline and admiration</b></li><li>Authority is limited by the natural responsibilities - noblesse obligee</li></ul>
What is paternalism?	<ul><li>Government by the people who are best equipt to lead, by virtue of their birth, inheritance or upbringing.&nbsp;</li><li>Social elite provides leadership, just as a father exercises authority - in the interest of the people.&nbsp;</li><li>Those at the top have a duty of care - noblesse obligee.</li></ul>
What are the two forms of paternalism	<ul><li>Soft - when those who are recipients give their consent.&nbsp;</li><li>Hard - when paternalism is imposed, regardless of consent or oppoition, in a more authoritarian manner.</li></ul>
How did the moderate Conservative governments of 1950s and 60s adopt one nation conservative policeis?	<ul><li>Adopted a 'middle way' (Macmillan) between free market economics and state planning.&nbsp;</li><li>Markets failed to protect poorest and created social fragmentation, whilst planning stiffled entrepreneurical flair.&nbsp;</li><li>Harold Macmillan called it 'private enterprise without selfishness'.&nbsp;</li></ul>
