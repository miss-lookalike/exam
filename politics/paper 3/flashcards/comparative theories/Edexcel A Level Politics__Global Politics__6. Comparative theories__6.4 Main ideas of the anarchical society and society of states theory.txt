What is the anarchical society and society of states theory&nbsp;	<ul><li>Written by Hedley Bull in 1997. Foundling text of the English school of international relations.&nbsp;</li><li>Theory that the states of the world can be members of a society, despite the anarchical nature of the international system.&nbsp;</li><li>Accepts the neo-realist premise that there is anarchy in the international system.&nbsp;</li><li>But, among the chaos, a&nbsp;<b>society of states have emerged.&nbsp;</b></li><ul><li>States acknowledge that they have common interests.&nbsp;<br></li><li>So they develop “common rules and institutions” to mitigate this issue</li></ul></ul>
What does Bull argue about international anarchy?&nbsp;	<ul><li>Accepts that states do not have any higher authority, therefore exist in a condition of international anarchy.&nbsp;</li><li>However,&nbsp;<b>unlike realists,&nbsp;</b>this does inevitably create a struggle for power that can only be constrained by a balance of power.&nbsp;</li><li>Conflict can be&nbsp;<b>avoided&nbsp;</b>by the existance of a&nbsp;<b>society of states.&nbsp;</b></li><li>Seen as a form of&nbsp;<b>liberal realism.&nbsp;</b><br></li></ul>
Why do states have an understanding that ensures that states have a degree of co-operation.&nbsp;	<ul><li>States are aware of shared interests and values so develop institutions, rules and values by which they agree to be bound.&nbsp;</li><li>Alternative forms of organisation exist, such as regional and international organisations, but state co-operation privdes the best cahnce of achieving order in world politics.&nbsp;</li><li><b>Disadvantages of the theory&nbsp;</b></li><li>Falls short of the idea of global governance or supernationalism.&nbsp;<br></li><li>Does not account for the developement of supranational bodies, such as the World Trade Organisation or the International Criminal Court.&nbsp;</li></ul>
Expanded analysis of the anarchical society theory&nbsp;	"<div>
<div>
<div>
<div><ul><li>Bull argued that although nation-states still play the dominant role in international relations, this does not mean that there must therefore be constant war and conflict.</li><li>This is because nation-states, although they are still sovereign, choose to cooperate with other nation-states to achieve positive outcomes for
themselves. (Joseph Nye, positive sum, 2010)</li><li><div>
<div>
<div>
<div>
<div>Global politics is still anarchic because there is no supranational
body that can compel obedience. However, there are still numerous
channels through which nation-states can choose to cooperate rather
than compete.</div>
</div>
</div>
</div></div></li><li><div>As a result, it is possible to create order and stability out of global
anarchy. By seeing mutual advantages in cooperation, a society (rather
than just a system) of states is created.</div></li><li><div><div>
<div>
<div>
<div>
<div>It is possible for there to be an <span style=""font-weight: 700; color: rgb(77, 77, 255);"">anarchical society of states</span>. This is
because although sovereign nation-states remain the key actors in
global politics, they generally choose to act according to accepted rules
of international behaviour and abide by international law.&nbsp;</div>
</div>
</div>
</div></div></div></li><li><div><div>
<div>
<div>
<div>
<div>Anarchical society theory attempts to bridge the gap between realist
and liberal interpretations of global politics.&nbsp;</div>
</div>
</div>
</div></div></div></li><li><div><div>
<div>
<div>
<div>
<div>By accepting the nation-state as the key actor in global relations,
anarchical society is within the realist tradition. However, by arguing
that nation-states generally choose to cooperate, it challenges the
realist emphasis on state egoism, placing it within a more liberal
interpretation of international relations.&nbsp;</div>
</div>
