What does hegemonic stability theory suggest is the impact of unipolarity? (Neo-realists) or neocons	<ul><li>Unipolarity can lead to international peace and security if the hegemon is well regarded as a benign hegemon.&nbsp;</li><li>1) This is because other states will '<b>bandwagon</b>' behind the hegemon</li><li>2) As the dominant power the hegemon can be relied upon to <b>maintain the status quo</b> through constructive utilisation of its global influence</li><li>3) The hegemon can also act as the '<b>world's police officer</b>' by intervening in conflict and setting the ground rules for economic behaviour.&nbsp;</li></ul>
Why do neo-realists see unipolarity as the natural consequence of states seeking power in an anarchic system?	<ul><li>Because the surest way to protect the state from threats is to become the dominant power or hegemon</li></ul>
Why do liberals reject the view that unipolarity promotes global peace?	<ul><li>Because they fear the emergence of a predatory hegemon that desires power at all costs.</li><li>No checks and balances on their power&nbsp;</li><li>Other powers will come to fear the megalomania of the dominant power, creating a security dialema.&nbsp;</li><li>Process of achieving dominance involves a power struggle that creates hostility and conflict</li></ul>
What is Noam Chomsky's malign hegemon theory?	<ul><li>Chomsky argues that unipolarity destabilises global relations because a hegemon can act in defiance of international laws and conventions.&nbsp;</li><li>E.g. American foreign policy during the administration of George W Bush when the US acted in defiance of UN</li></ul>

Why do realists like Kenneth Waltz argue that bipolarity provides the greatest potential for global stability?	<ul><li>Because the aggressive impulses of each of the two superpowers will be restricted by the other, due to mutually assured distruction.&nbsp;</li><li>The rivalry between the two states will lead to a global balance of power or equilibrium</li></ul>
Why do liberals criticise bipolarity?	<ul><li>Bipolarity leads to an arms race as states fight to become the dominant power through an arms race.&nbsp;</li><li>This leads to tension, insecurity and nuclear proliferation.&nbsp;</li><ul><li>Encourages distrust and suspicion - Cuban Missile Crisis almost led to nuclear disaster</li><li>Numerous proxy wars in Korea, Vietnam, Afriacnistan&nbsp;</li></ul><li>There will also come a time when circumstances allow one to emerge as the dominant power.&nbsp;</li></ul>
Why did the era of bipolarity end after the cold war?	<ul><li>Soviety Union was not able to achieve economic parity.&nbsp;</li><li>Resources were diverted for arms and space races and consumer goods declined. Potential for econoimc growth was squandered.&nbsp;</li><li>The Soviets could not match US increases in military spending. Therefore had to embrace glasnost and perestroika</li></ul>
Why do realists and liberals disagree on bipolarity?	<ul><li>Realists favour bipolarity because they believe it encourages equilibrium, balance and stability by disincentivising conflict.&nbsp;</li><li>Liberals argue that bipolarity is based on mutual suspicion, fear and resentment, so does not provide foundations for a lasting peace</li></ul>
