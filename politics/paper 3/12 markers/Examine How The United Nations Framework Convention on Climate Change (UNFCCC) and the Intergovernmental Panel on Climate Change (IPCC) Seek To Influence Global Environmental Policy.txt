Paragraph One
Point Of Comparison
One way in which both the UNFCCC and the IPCC seek to influence global environmental policy
is by stressing the potentially catastrophic consequences of climate change and the need for
immediate action.
The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change UNFCCC

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change IPCC
The IPCCʼs status as the most prominent scientific advisory body on climate change also means
it play an important role in stressing how dangerous the impending climate catastrophe is and
the need for immediate action.
Its detailed Assessment Reports synthesise findings from thousands of studies to convey
the realities of climate change to policymakers and the public.
The IPCCʼs work has helped to shift global public opinion on climate change, leading to
stronger calls for action. Campaigns like the Fridays for Future strikes initiated by Greta
Thunberg have drawn on IPCC findings to underscore the immediacy of the climate threat
and rally public support.
Paragraph Two
Point Of Comparison
Another important way that both the UNFCCC and the IPCC influence global environmental
policy is by making policy recommendations that are implemented by national governments.
The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change UNFCCC
Through brokering international agreements like the Kyoto Protocol and Paris Agreement, the
UNFCCC provides a framework for countries to set climate goals and implement policies. These
agreements guide national strategies and ensure action against climate change.
For example, the Paris Agreement introduced Nationally Determined Contributions NDCs),
which require countries to outline and update their plans for reducing emissions.
This mechanism influenced Germanyʼs Climate Action Law, which set binding targets to
achieve carbon neutrality by 2045, showcasing the UNFCCCʼs role in shaping national
climate frameworks.
The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change IPCC
The IPCCʼs policy recommendations also play an important role in influencing national climate
policies, as their independent scientific analysis gives legitimacy to government policies and
laws.
For example, the UKʼs Climate Change Act of 2008, which set legally binding targets for
emissions reductions, was strongly influenced by IPCC findings on the link between carbon
emissions and global warming.

Point Of Comparison

fostering international consensus and collaboration on tackling climate change.

The UNFCCC has institutionalised climate discussions, ensuring that climate change remains a
top priority on the international agenda. COPs have become crucial events where countries
come together to negotiate agreements and assess collective progress.
Not only has this facilitated the signing of international agreements such as the The
Copenhagen Accord 2009 and Paris Agreement 2015, it has also held countries to
account by establishing a system for countries to report their emissions.
For example, Japan received criticism after scaling back its emissions targets in the
wake of the Fukushima nuclear disaster in 2011, as it increased reliance on fossil fuels.
Further, the UNFCCC has created mechanisms such as the Green Climate Fund GCF to
support developing countries in both mitigating and adapting to climate change. The GCF,
established in 2010, aimed to provide $100 billion annually by 2020 from developed to
developing nations.
The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change IPCC
The IPCCʼs reports have also fostered international collaboration and action, as they have
informed major climate agreements, including the most recent Paris Agreement.
The Fourth Assessment Report 2007 and Fifth Assessment Report 2014 highlighted the
scientific basis for immediate action and the potentially catastrophic consequences of
allowing global warming to rise by 2°C.
This informed the Paris Agreementʼs target to limit global warming to “well belowˮ 2°C and
ideally to 1.5°C.
