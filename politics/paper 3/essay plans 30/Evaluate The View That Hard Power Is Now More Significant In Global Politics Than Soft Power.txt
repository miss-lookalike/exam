Evaluate The View That Hard Power Is Now More Significant In Global Politics Than Soft Power.

Introduction
Key Definitions
Hard power refers to the use of coercive measures, typically involving military force or
economic sanctions, to compel others to act in ways they otherwise would not. It is based on
tangible resources, such as military strength or economic leverage.
Soft power, by contrast, is the ability to shape the preferences and opinions of others through
appeal and attraction rather than coercion. It relies on cultural influence, diplomacy, and moral
authority to achieve influence without force.

Paragraph One ➡ Upholding International Norms
Against: Soft Power Leads To Long-Term Influence And Global Stability
One important argument that soft power is in fact more significant than hard power in global
politics is that it continues to uphold international norms and provide global stability, both
through international governmental organisations IGOs) and multilateral cooperation between
states.
By appealing to shared values, cultural ties, and international norms, soft power fosters
trust and cooperation between states, creating a more stable and predictable international
environment.
The European Union offers economic incentives and promotes shared European values to
encourage nations to strengthen their democratic institutions and align with international
human rights standards.
For example, prior to Russiaʼs invasion the EU provided extensive financial aid and
institutional support to Ukraine through its Association Agreement, encouraging judicial
reforms and anti-corruption measures to strengthen democratic governance and align
with EU standards. Ukraine was incentivised to do so as it moved it closer to potential
future EU membership.
Similarly, The African Union AU employs soft power to promote peace and democracy in
Africa by deploying election observers and mediating political conflicts, as seen in their
efforts to mediate the political crisis in Mali in 2020.
By focusing on African unity and shared regional interests, the AU fosters cooperation
and stability without the need for military intervention.
The use of hard power fails to do so and instead leads to resentment that can come back to
damage a stateʼs interests.
This can be seen in the fact that the USʼ intervention in Afghanistan during the Cold War
supported the Mujahideen, which had strong connections to Al Qaeda that later emerged
and attacked the US on 9/11, including the fact that they were both funded by Osama Bin
Laden.
Similar unintended effects were seen in the United Statesʼ attempt to impose democracy
through military interventions in Afghanistan and Iraq, both of which failed. This suggests
that soft power is in fact the only way to promote a more stable and liberal international
order.

************************************************************************************************

For: Hard Power Is Sometimes The Only Way To Uphold International Norms
On the other hand, it can be argued that hard power is now more important soft power in global
politics as the past few years have proven it is the only way to uphold international norms and
defend democracy/human rights when a rogue state seeks to challenge them using their own
hard power.
It is often the only effective response when dealing with actors that do not respond to
diplomacy or persuasion and it can be argued that if states arenʼt prepared to use hard
power to protect international norms, then these international norms are extremely weak.
After Russia invaded Ukraine in 2022, the European Union, United Kingdom and United States
in particular didnʼt sit back, instead they sought to protect Ukrainian sovereignty by providing
military equipment, funding and sanctioning the Russian economy. Without this support, it is
unlikely that Ukraine would have been able to hold of Russiaʼs advances for over two years.
Immediately after the invasion, the EU imposed sweeping economic sanctions on Russia,
targeting its financial system, energy exports, and key individuals close to President Putin.
These measures included freezing the assets of Russian banks, banning energy imports like
oil and coal, and restricting Russiaʼs access to global financial markets.
In 2022 alone, the United States, European Union, and United Kingdom collectively
committed over $60 billion in military, humanitarian, and financial aid to Ukraine.
Similarly, Iranʼs advancement of its nuclear programme in the 2010s has threatened global
nuclear non-proliferation. Whilst Obama and Biden sought a diplomatic solution through the Iran
Nuclear Deal JCPOA, Trump and Netanyahu in particular judged that the use of hard power
would be more effective.
In 2018, Trump withdrew the US from the JCPOA and reimposed severe economic sanctions
on Iran. These sanctions targeted critical sectors of the Iranian economy, including oil
exports, banking, and shipping. The goal was to pressure Iran into renegotiating its nuclear
program and curbing its regional influence.
Israel sees Iranʼs nuclear capabilities as an existential threat and has as a result repeatedly
used military power to disrupt Iranʼs nuclear program through covert airstrikes and targeted
assassinations. As it distrusts Iran, it believes this is the only way to halt its nuclear
programme.
Israel is believed to be behind explosions at Iranʼs Natanz nuclear facility in 2020 and
2021, which significantly delayed Iranʼs uranium enrichment capabilities.
In addition, the assassinations of key Iranian nuclear scientists, including Mohsen
Fakhrizadeh in 2020, further undermined Iranʼs progress toward developing nuclear
weapons.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, there is no doubt that soft power is central to promoting global stability and upholding
international norms in the long-term, however without being willing to use hard power to uphold
these norms then they are largely worthless.
Combined with the growing number of hard power actions by states such as Russia that needed
to be defended with hard power themselves, this therefore means that hard power is more
significant than soft power in global politics.

Paragraph Two ➡ Achieving A Stateʼs Aims
Against: Soft Power Enhances A Stateʼs Global Reputation and Legitimacy
Another argument against the view that hard more is now more significant than soft power is
that soft power enhances a stateʼs global reputation and legitimacy, whilst hard power often
damages it and limits a countryʼs long-term influence.
States that successfully wield soft power are often seen as significant mediating states in a
multipolar world, which can translate into increased influence in global governance and
decision-making. A state with strong soft power can shape international outcomes and lead
global initiatives through persuasion rather than force.
In the past couple of years, Saudi Arabia has significantly expanded its global influence and
prominence through its effective use of soft power. It has been able to do so by using its
strength as an oil producer to develop relatively friendly relations with both Western
countries like the U.S. and powers who challenge the U.S. like China and Russia.
Its soft power influence can be seen in the fact that in February 2025 it hosted peace
talks between the U.S., Ukraine and Russia in relation to the war in Ukraine.
It can also be seen in Saudi Arabiaʼs heavy investment in sport to enhance its global
profile, including investing over $4.5 billion in the LIV golf league and successfully
bidding to host the 2034 Fifa World Cup.
Soft can be seen as particularly important for global superpowers, which can use cultural
influence to spread their values and enhance their global reputation and a worthy and
benevolent power.
The global reach of Hollywood films has been a key source of soft power for the United
States. Through movies and television shows, American values such as individualism,
democracy, and freedom are projected globally, shaping how people view the U.S.
These cultural exports create positive associations with the U.S. and influence
international audiences, making them more receptive to American policies and
ideals.
The 2008 Beijing Olympics was a strategic use of soft power by China to enhance its
international image. Through a highly publicised and successful event, China
showcased its cultural heritage, modern infrastructure, and rising global status.
The games were viewed by billions worldwide, helping to shift international
perceptions of China as a powerful and emerging global player that should be taken
seriously.
In contrast, hard power can damage a stateʼs reputation and erode its legitimacy. The limitations
of hard power in this context are apparent when coercion leads to accusations of imperialism,
human rights abuses, or unjust aggression.
States that rely too heavily on military interventions or economic sanctions often face global
backlash, losing credibility and weakening their ability to influence others through
attraction.
This can be seen in the reduced legitimacy and support received by the Israeli state after it
killed over 40,000 in the Israel-Gaza war, including European leaders openly criticising
Netanyahu when they hadnʼt before.

************************************************************************************************

For: Hard Power Can Lead To Immediate Results For A State Even If Its Actions
Are Unpopular
On the other hand, it can be argued that hard power is now more significant than soft power, as
states as the weakening of human rights and the USʼ reputation globally means that states are
now more willing to use hard power to get immediate results for themselves.
Even if this may do some damage to their reputation and long-term soft power, they can
simply point to the hypocrisy of the United States after its human rights abuses and invasion
of Iraq as part of the War on Terror, under the false pretence that Saddam Hussein had
weapons of mass destruction.
Hard power is unrivalled, as coercive measures can compel other actors to comply swiftly and
achieve goals such as enlarging its territory or changing a regime. It provides a direct means to
change behaviour when diplomacy or cultural influence fails to produce results.
This can be seen in Russiaʼs invasion of Ukraine in 2022, which was aimed at overthrowing
the Ukrainian government, asserting control over Ukrainian territory, and preventing Ukraine
from moving closer to Western institutions like NATO and the European Union.
Putin would have had no chance of pursuing its goals of enlarging Russiaʼs territory
through diplomatic/soft power means and therefore utilised hard power when it wasnʼt
expected.
Russia also used economic power to support its war aims, by cutting off or reducing gas
supplies to countries like Germany, Poland, and others that supported Ukraine. This led to a
severe energy crisis in Europe, causing a spike in energy prices and forcing European
governments to seek alternative energy sources.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, it is clear that soft power gives a state power long-term influence but sometimes it is
insufficient to achieve the immediate aims of a state. Both types of power remain equally
important to achieving state aims and should be combined as ‘smart powerʼ to achieve a stateʼs
aims most effectively, with a balance of the two dependent on the particular goals.

Paragraph Three ➡ Gaining Support From Citizens
Against: Soft Power Is Far Less Costly And Controversial In Domestic Politics
A final argument that hard power isnʼt in fact more significant than soft power is that it carries
far more risks to politicians domestically and is therefore often avoided.
Military interventions and sanctions can lead to enormous economic and human costs.
Wars and economic blockades often lead to prolonged suffering, not only for the targeted
state but also for the citizens of the initiator, which can also undermine a governmentʼs
support with its citizens.
The 2003 U.S. invasion of Iraq resulted in high civilian casualties and long-term
instability in the region, costing the U.S. over $2 trillion and contributing to public fatigue
with prolonged military interventions.
This economic burden and loss of life led to significant criticism domestically,
undermining support for George Bushʼs government and playing a significant role in
the Democrats winning the Presidency and Congress in the 2008 elections.
Hard power also carries the risk of entangling states in long, drawn-out conflicts or
economic retaliation that undermine their initial objectives.
It could be argued that this has been the case with Russiaʼs invasion of Ukraine, which
has got them bogged down in a long war that seems unlikely to be won by either side,
whilst over 70,000 Russian soldiers have died.
By contrast, soft power can pursue national interests without the financial and human costs and
the long-term consequences.
For example, Japan has effectively used its cultural exports, particularly anime and manga,
to enhance its soft power globally. These art forms have gained immense popularity,
fostering positive perceptions of Japanese culture and encouraging economic relationships
and tourism which boost the Japanese economy.

************************************************************************************************

For: Hard Power Is Vital For Providing Deterrence and Security
A final argument that hard power is more significant, however, is that it is vital to providing
deterrence and security for a state, which is the most important priority for a state and most of
its citizens.
Hard power is essential for deterrence, where the mere threat of military or economic retaliation
can prevent aggression. A strong military or economic position acts as a safeguard against
potential attacks, ensuring that other states calculate the high costs of coercive actions against
a powerful state.
NATOʼs collective defence policy, backed by the military power and nuclear capabilities of
the United States, France and United Kingdom, serves as a deterrent against aggression
toward smaller European countries. Knowing that an attack on one member will result in a
unified military response makes adversaries think twice before acting against any NATO
nation.
Similarly, Israelʼs advanced defence systems and its strategic alliances with nations like the
United States deter neighbouring states from attempting direct military action.
The countryʼs demonstrated military capability, along with its intelligence network,
raises the stakes for any hostile action, reducing the likelihood of large-scale attacks.
Hard power therefore creates a security buffer that soft power cannot, as cultural and
diplomatic influence alone are insufficient to protect states from external threats.
In the last few years, it has become clear that hard power is increasingly important in a
multipolar world where numerous powerful states are vying for global supremacy.
This can be seen in Trumpʼs threat to take Greenland militarily and agreement in April 2025
to deploy U.S. troops along the Panama Canal to assert U.S. power and tackle growing
Chinese influence in the region.
The U.S. sees its dominance in the Western Hemisphere as strategically important for its
security and is willing to sacrifice some of its reputation to guarantee it.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, hard power carries far more risks than soft power but it is vital for deterrence and
sometimes canʼt be avoided, particularly in an increasingly multipolar and fractious world with
growing conflict.
Though soft power remains important, hard power has therefore become more significant in
recent years as global threats have changed.

In conclusion, it is clear that both soft power and hard power remain important in global politics
and that states should combine both through ‘smart powerʼ to achieve their aims and uphold
international norms.
