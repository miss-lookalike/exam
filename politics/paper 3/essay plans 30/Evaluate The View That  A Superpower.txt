Introduction
Key Definitions
A superpower is a state that possesses the ability to project major power across the global
across military, economic, political and cultural dimensions.

Paragraph One ➡ Economic Power
For: China Is Now A Superpower
The most persuasive argument that China is now a superpower is that its economic might now
rivals the United Statesʼ and will soon take it over to become the worldʼs largest economy.
Chinaʼs economic growth over the past four decades has been extraordinary, making it the
worldʼs second-largest economy with a nominal GDP of approximately $19 trillion in 2024. It is
expected to become the worldʼs largest economy by 2035.
Central to this has been its dominance of global manufacturing since joining the World
Trade Organisation WTO in 2001. China accounts for 30% of global output, making it the
worldʼs manufacturing hub.
It is deeply entrenched in global supply chains, with many countries and corporations
dependent on Chinese production, and as a result this economic power will be near
impossible to weaken despite US attempts to decouple from China.
Importantly, this economic power also supports Chinaʼs superpower status as it is an important
player in geopolitically important sectors and many other countriesʼ economies are dependent
on China.
China is increasingly competitive in sectors such as 5G technology, artificial intelligence,
biotechnology and electric vehicles, all of which are central to geopolitical competition and
economic power in the 21st century.
Chinese companies like Huawei, Tencent and electric vehicle manufacturer BYD are
huge, technologically advanced companies that are scaring the US into imposing
sanctions.
Particularly important was the gain main by AI company Deepseek in early 2025,
which shocked U.S. competitors like Nvidia who thought they dominated the market,
resulting in a major fall in their stock price.
This growing power in high-tech industries was intentionally planned by the Made In
China 2025 Plan, introduced in 2015, which heavily invested in research & development
to make China a global leader in high value industries to challenge the US and reduce
reliance on the West.
China is also the worldʼs largest exporter and the largest trading partner for more than 120
countries, including many in Asia, Africa, and Europe.
It is able to leverage this economic influence for geopolitical gain and to destabilise the
West, as can be seen in the fact that Hungary has become an important supporter of its
influence within the European Union, therefore causing rifts in a key economic
competitor.

************************************************************************************************

Against: China Isnʼt A Superpower
On the other hand, it can be argued that Chinaʼs economic growth is vulnerable and doesnʼt yet
constitute superpower status.
Though its economic growth so far has been remarkable, it is still heavily reliant on exports and
investment and there are numerous challenges that suggest it may not be able to continue this
economic success.
China has very high corporate and local government debt, which exceeded 280% of GDP in
recent years.
Moreover, Chinaʼs economy is confronting a significant demographic challenge: its
population is aging rapidly, with a shrinking workforce.
This is coupled with a slowing growth rate, which fell to below 5% in 2023, a far cry
from the double-digit growth rates of the 1990s and 2000s.
These factors cast doubt on Chinaʼs long-term ability to sustain the economic power
necessary to become and global superpower and challenge the United States fully.
Further, it can also be argued that China isnʼt yet an economic superpower on the same level as
the United States because it lacks a number of the structural factors that support the USʼ
economic dominance.
The US dollar is the worldʼs reserve currency, accounting for nearly 60% of global reserves.
This ensures American influence in global trade and financial systems and gives it
significant influence over other countriesʼ economic success.
US companies also continue to be the worldʼs biggest brands and it has the worldʼs most
advanced and highly regarded universities, which have a global reputation built over
decades.
7 out of the top 10 global brands and 61 out of the top 100 global brands are American.
These include Amazon, Apple, Google and Tesla.
Though Chinaʼs currency is growing in power, it has a growing number of global brands and
good universities, it can be argued that it still lags behind significantly in these areas, which
undermine its ability to challenge the US as a superpower.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, Chinaʼs economy doesnʼt yet have the structural support of the United Statesʼ but with
it soon to be the worldʼs largest economy and the potential for significant further growth, there
is no doubt that it has enough economic power to be considered a superpower.

Paragraph Two ➡ Military Power
For: China Is Now A Superpower
China can also be argued to now be a superpower as it has translated its economic power and
huge population into significant economic power, now making it the worldʼs second most
powerful military.
China has invested heavily in modernising its military, and by 2024, it has the worldʼs second-
largest defence budget at $230 billion.
The Peopleʼs Liberation Army PLA has made significant advances in naval power, with the
Chinese navy now boasting the worldʼs largest number of ships.
China has also developed advanced missile technology, including hypersonic missiles and
ballistic missile systems.
These advancements in military technology and power mean that it is now able to project
significant power globally and has especially increased its assertiveness in the Indo-Pacific
region to challenge the US military.
China has sought to gain control over the South China Sea by militarising existing islands
and creating artificial islands to be military bases. This is highly important due to its
centrality to global shipping routes and its proximity to China.
With over 1.4 billion people, it must also be recognised that China has significant latent military
power if it needed to mobilise its forces for war.

************************************************************************************************

Against: China Isnʼt A Superpower
On the other hand, it can be argued that China isnʼt yet a superpower as its military power still
lags significantly behind the United States in several critical areas.
The U.S. defence budget, at $997 billion 2024, dwarfs Chinaʼs, and the U.S. military
maintains a global presence through over 700 military bases worldwide compared to
Chinaʼs less than 5.
The technological gap between the U.S. and China in terms of military equipment, training,
and battlefield experience also remains wide.
China has not engaged in large-scale military operations on the level of the United
States, which has decades of experience in global interventions and warfare.
Additionally, while Chinaʼs military is formidable in its region and the South China Sea, it lacks
the expeditionary capabilities of the U.S., which can project military power anywhere in the
world with unparalleled logistics, naval assets, and air power.
This ability to project power globally is arguably a key condition for being a superpower and
it can therefore be argued that Chinaʼs lack of military strength means it is not yet a
superpower.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, the most persuasive argument is that China isnʼt yet a superpower. One of the key
conditions of being a superpower is the ability to project military power globally and China isnʼt
yet able to do so, with its military funding, size and capabilities still dwarfed by that of the
United States as all countries are.

Paragraph Three ➡ Geopolitical Power and Allies
For: China Is Now A Superpower
A final argument that China should now be considered a superpower is that it has a number of
important allies that bolster its challenge to US supremacy and that it has significant geopolitical
power.
China has formed strong partnerships with Russia, North Korea and Iran, seen both in economic
cooperation and joint military alliances. This supports Chinese power for two main reasons.
Firstly, both Russia and Iran (which exports 90% of its oil to China) provide China with vital
oil exports to support its continued economic growth.
Secondly, they share common goals to reduce US influence globally, including in Eurasia
and the Middle East.
Chinaʼs President Xi has a clear vision to bring about the rejuvenation of the Chinese nation as a
global superpower. In the last decade in particular, China has pursued this by growing its
interdependence with countries in the Global South through the Belt and Road Initiative BRI.
The Belt and Road Initiative BRI was launched in 2013 to develop trade routes that link
China with countries across the world and by 2024 over 140 countries have joined the
initiative.
It is designed to deepen Chinaʼs economic ties with partner countries, and extend
Chinaʼs strategic influence through a large number of infrastructure projects in other
countries funded by Chinese capital and loans.
As a result of the initiative, it has been able to both achieve strategic objectives and grow its
economic importance in many countries, therefore increasing its leverage.
For example, the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor CPEC involves constructing
highways and energy projects between China and Pakistan.
This has not only helped China reduce its reliance on trade moving through the South
China Sea and helped it secure an alternative route for oil from the Middle East, it has
also given it leverage over the political decisions of Pakistanʼs government to ensure
they are in Chinaʼs interest.

************************************************************************************************

Against: China Isnʼt A Superpower
On the other hand, it can be argued that China isnʼt yet a superpower as it still lacks the deep
network of alliances that the United States maintains, particularly among great powers.
The U.S. leads alliances like NATO, which includes 30 member states, and bilateral defence
agreements with key players in the Indo-Pacific, such as Japan, South Korea, and Australia.
Further, the US has strong historical alliances with the European great powers of the
United Kingdom, France and Germany, as well as Japan. The economic power of these
allies far outweighs that of Russia and Iran.
In contrast, China has relatively few formal allies, and many of its relationships are
transactional or based on economic dependency, rather than strong military alliances.
In fact, many of Chinaʼs infrastructure projects involved in the Belt and Road Initiative
have led to it being accused of ‘dept-trap diplomacyʼ, where it gains strategic influence
after countries struggle to repay their loans, such as with Sri Lankaʼs Hambantota Port.
This has undermined Chinaʼs global reputation as it has been accused of exploiting
countriesʼ poverty and dependence for its own interests.
Additionally, Chinaʼs aggressive behaviour in areas like the South China Sea and its treatment of
Uyghurs in Xinjiang have decreased its soft powers and alienated many potential partners,
particularly in the West.
Countries such as India, which once saw China as a partner, have grown wary of its
intentions, particularly after the border clashes between Chinese and Indian troops in the
Himalayas.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, Chinaʼs geopolitical influence has grown and Russia and Iran are certainly important
allies, but at the moment it still lacks the deep network of allies and strong geopolitical influence
to be considered a superpower. The United States has strong historical relationships with many
countries and structural power in global governance institutions, which is difficult to dislodge.

************************************************************************************************
************************************************************************************************

Overall Argument
In conclusion, China isnʼt yet a superpower.
It has sufficient economic power to be considered a superpower and will no doubt be able to
use its future economic growth and large population to bolster its military power.
However, it currently lacks the military power to project its power globally or the strong network
of allies and geopolitical alliances that are required to be considered a superpower or truly
challenge the United States, which remains hegemonic in particular due to its incredible military
power and influence over other Western democracies.
