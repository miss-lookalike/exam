Evaluate The View That Globalisation Has Had A Positive Impact On The Environment. (30) Politics Explained

Introduction
Key Definitions
Globalisation today is understood as the complex web of interconnectedness that defines
global politics across multiple dimensions, including the economic, political and cultural
spheres. What happens in one nation state influences and is influenced by decisions and
factors across the world.

Paragraph One ➡ Economic Globalisation
For: Economic Globalisation Has Enabled Green Technology and Renewable
Energy
It can be argued that economic globalisation has had a positive impact on the environment, as it
has accelerated the development and distribution of green technology and renewable energy.
wind turbines, and electric vehicles have been able to spread rapidly across borders.
Further, as globalisation has been effective in creating rapid growth in China and other
countries, these countries have been able to play a central role in producing green
technology and relying less on fossil fuels than previous economic superpowers.
China has become a major producer of renewable energy technologies, driving down costs and
making these solutions more accessible worldwide.
For example, the global cost of solar energy has dropped by nearly 90% since 2010, largely
due to international competition and investment.
Foreign direct investment FDI and cross-border technology transfer have allowed many
developing countries to leapfrog fossil-fuel dependency and invest directly in clean energy,
contributing to reduced global emissions.

************************************************************************************************

Against: Economic Globalisation Has Led to Increased Carbon Emissions,
Pollution, Deforestation, and Resource Exploitation
On the other hand, it can be argued that economic globalisation has significantly contributed to
environmental degradation, as the pursuit of wealth and economic growth by corporations and
countries has come at the expense of the environment.
Global supply chains, driven by international trade and foreign direct investment FDI, have
increased industrial production, leading to higher carbon emissions from transportation,
manufacturing, and energy use.
The International Energy Agency IEA estimates that between 1990 and 2018, the global
energy sector saw a 50% increase in CO2 emissions, driven by industrial growth and global
trade.
Corporations, in their quest for profits, frequently relocate production to countries with weak
environmental regulations, where pollution controls are minimal. This has led to extensive
environmental pollution, including air and water contamination.
Moreover, the increased global demand for raw materials, such as oil, minerals, and timber, has
accelerated resource exploitation and deforestation.
A report released in late 2024 revealed that global deforestation continued to increase
across 2023, surging to 6.4 million hectares despite the fact that 140 countries pledged in
2021 to end deforestation by 2030.
Countries that saw a particular increase included Indonesia and Bolivia and the increase
was driven by global demand for beef, soy and palm oil in particular.
This is a clear example of the negative impact of economic globalisation on the
environment, as demands from across the world for resources and the potential profits that
countries can make stand in the way of real progress on climate change.
The global economyʼs focus on extractive industries has also driven overfishing, depletion of
soil, and the mining of non-renewable resources, exacerbating environmental damage and
undermining the sustainability of ecosystems.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, in the unlikely event that the economic globalisation will lead to the invention of
technology that solves climate change then it will have a positive impact on the environment. To
date, however, globalisation has had an incredibly detrimental impact on the environment and
will likely only continue to do so.

Paragraph Two ➡ Political Globalisation
For: Political Globalisation Has Helped Coordinate Action Against Climate
Change
Perhaps the most persuasive argument that globalisation has had a positive impact on the
environment is that political globalisation has been important in fostering global cooperation to
tackle climate change. International institutions like the UN and treaties like the Paris Agreement
have brought nations together to commit to reducing carbon emissions.
The Paris Agreement, adopted in 2015, commits countries to keep the global temperature rise
this century well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels. 196 countries have signed the
agreement, representing nearly the entire world.
It also aims to strengthen the ability of countries to deal with the impacts of climate change
and requires nations to submit Nationally Determined Contributions NDCs) outlining their
plans to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.
There has also been some recognition from developed countries about how they have caused
climate change and that they should assist poorer countries to help tackle it.
At the COP26 Summit in Glasgow in 2021 developed countries reaffirmed their commitment
to provide $100 billion annually to support developing nations in mitigating and adapting to
the impacts of climate change.

************************************************************************************************

Against: Political Globalisation Has Been Ineffective in Getting Nation-States to
Tackle Climate Change
On the other hand, while political globalisation has facilitated international discussions on
climate change, it must be recognised that it has largely been ineffective in compelling nation-
states to take decisive action. Despite international agreements such as the Paris Agreement,
many countries have fallen short of their emission reduction targets.
The nature of international agreements allows for voluntary commitments, and the lack of
enforceable mechanisms has meant that countries can set inadequate goals or fail to meet
their commitments without significant consequences.
For instance, as of 2025, several major emitters, including the United States and China,
have failed to meet their Nationally Determined Contributions NDCs) under the Paris
Agreement.
The U.S. initially committed to reducing its emissions by 2628% below 2005 levels by
2025. However, political challenges, in particular as the U.S. withdrawal from the Paris
Agreement during both Trump administrations, as well as Trumpʼs strong support for
domestic oil production, have hindered progress.
Furthermore, global political structures have been unable to overcome the inherent tensions
between developed and developing nations. Developed nations, historically responsible for the
bulk of greenhouse gas emissions, have been slow to meet their financial commitments to help
poorer countries mitigate and adapt to climate change.
Many developing nations argue that these financial commitments are insufficient and
delayed, leaving them vulnerable to the worst effects of climate change. This failure to
foster genuine international cooperation has hindered the effectiveness of political
globalisation in addressing the climate crisis.
At COP26, the $100 billion annual commitment by developed countries was reaffirmed, but
there has been widespread criticism over the lack of follow-through since it was first made
at COP15 in 2009.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, it is clear that political globalisation has failed to stop economic globalisation destroying
the environment. Whilst the Paris Agreement was certainly important, it was far from radical
enough to meaningfully tackle climate change, whilst it has failed to compel states to truly
tackle climate change, as they continue to prioritise economic growth.

Paragraph Three ➡ Cultural Globalisation
For: Cultural Globalisation Has Led to Global Social Movements Against
Climate Change
A final argument that globalisation has had a positive impact on the environment is that cultural
globalisation has facilitated widespread awareness of environmental issues.
The rapid spread of information through the internet and social media has allowed
environmental movements to gain global traction, giving a voice to marginalised groups and
youth activists, particularly from the Global South, who otherwise might struggle to be
heard on the international stage.
These platforms have democratised access to global conversations, enabling grassroots
movements to flourish and increasing public pressure on governments and corporations to
prioritise climate action.
A notable example is the Fridays for Future movement, started by Swedish climate activist Greta
Thunberg in 2018. Thunbergʼs solitary school strikes quickly gained international attention
through social media, inspiring millions of young people to join climate strikes across the globe.
The movement united young people from diverse regions, including Africa, Latin America, and
Asia, in calling for stronger and more immediate action on climate change.
The Fridays for Future movement was influential in pushing for stronger commitments at
major environmental summits, such as the 2019 UN Climate Action Summit and the COP26
conference. These IGOs were urged to adopt more ambitious climate policies, such as
targeting net-zero emissions and intensifying global efforts to limit temperature rise.
The movement also placed immense pressure on multinational corporations to enhance
their sustainability efforts. Many businesses faced public scrutiny and were compelled to
take more concrete steps to reduce their carbon footprints.
Major corporations like Amazon, Microsoft, and Unilever publicly announced new
climate goals, such as achieving carbon neutrality or adopting green energy initiatives.
In many cases, companies recognised that failing to respond to the demands of this
global climate movement would result in reputational damage and potential loss of
market share, especially among younger consumers who prioritise environmental
responsibility.

************************************************************************************************

Against: These Social Movements Have Been Largely Ineffective In Causing
Meaningful Changes To Tackle Climate Change
On the other hand it can be argued that the positive impact of cultural globalisation is limited, as
the influence of social movements is limited.
Despite global climate protests, governments and corporations continue to prioritise
economic growth over environmental protection, leading to insufficient action on climate
change.
Corporate greenwashing—where companies make superficial environmental claims without
substantive changes—has also become more common, with many businesses making token
gestures to satisfy public demands while continuing harmful practices.
For example, BP rebranded itself as “Beyond Petroleumˮ and launched campaigns
highlighting investments in renewable energy, but the company still allocates the vast
majority of its spending to oil and gas exploration and production.
A clear example of the gap between social movementsʼ demands and actual policy outcomes
can be seen in the agreements at COP26 in 2021. Despite intense pressure from environmental
activists who called for more urgent action, the final agreements fell short of the movementʼs
key goals.
Activists had campaigned for a firm commitment to limit global temperature rise to 1.5°C
and called for an immediate phase-out of coal, stronger financial commitments for climate
adaptation in developing countries, and a complete end to fossil fuel subsidies.
However, the Glasgow Climate Pact only included a pledge to “phase downˮ rather than
“phase outˮ coal, and the financial aid for developing nations was far below the requested
$100 billion per year.
These compromises, particularly around fossil fuels and financial support, were seen as a
disappointment by activists, who viewed the outcomes as insufficient to meet the scale of
the climate crisis.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, it is clear that the global social movements against climate change facilitated by cultural
globalisation have had a limited real impact on the environment, because of the fact that
companies and governments still prioritise their economic interests.

************************************************************************************************
************************************************************************************************

Overall Argument
In conclusion, globalisation has had an overwhelmingly negative impact on the environment.
The spread of global capitalism and the pursuit of wealth across the globe has come at the
expense of the environment, driving higher CO2 emissions, overfishing, destruction of
ecosystems and many other detrimental effects.
The IGOs of political globalisation and the social movements facilitated by cultural globalisation
have sought to put pressure on governments and companies to reduce their environmental
impact, but have largely failed due to the fact that economic success and the pursuit of profit
continues to be their primary motive.
