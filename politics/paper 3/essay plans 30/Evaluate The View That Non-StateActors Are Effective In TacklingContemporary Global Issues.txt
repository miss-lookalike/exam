Key Definitions
Contemporary global issues are defined as poverty, conflict, human rights and the environment.
These are the most prominent problems across the world that the international community

Paragraph One ➡ NGOs and Charitable Foundations
For: Non-State Actors Are Effective In Tackling Contemporary Global Issues
Two of the most important non-state actors that tackle contemporary global issues are
international non governmental organisations NGOs) and charitable foundations. Both have
significant resources and power which they can leverage to address systemic issues and
support vulnerable populations.Evaluate The View That Non-State Actors Are Effective In Tackling Contemporary Global Issues. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan1
NGOs are particularly effective in responding to crises and addressing human rights issues, as
their operational flexibility allows them to bypass bureaucratic delays and provide immediate
aid.
For example, during the Syrian Civil War, Doctors Without Borders delivered life-saving care
by setting up field hospitals and mobile clinics in hard-to-reach areas, providing essential
medical services amidst ongoing conflict.
Other NGOs such as Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch also protect human
rights by documenting human rights abuses and advocating for policy change from the
international community.
For example, Amnestyʼs reports on forced labor in Xinjiang prompted sanctions from the
EU and US, while Human Rights Watchʼs work on Rohingya abuses in Myanmar led to
international legal action.
They are able to do so not only as they mobilise public opinion and civil society, but also
because they hold some important roles in international governmental organisations,
such as Human Rights Watch having consultative status with the United Nations
Economic and Social Council ECOSOC, which allows it to attend and participate in UN
meetings, make written and oral statements, and engage with UN bodies on human
rights issues.
Charitable foundations also play an important role in tackling global issues, especially global
poverty through strategic interventions using their significant funding.
The Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, for example, has invested billions in initiatives such as
GAVI, the Vaccine Alliance, which has provided vaccines to more than 1 billion children in
low-income regions, preventing an estimated 13 million deaths from vaccine-preventable
diseases.
Its funding for agricultural development also supports food security by promoting
drought-resistant crops in Africa.
Similarly, the Ford Foundation empowers marginalised communities through microfinance
initiatives, providing access to credit for small businesses in countries like Bangladesh and
India, which enhances economic resilience.
Against: Non-State Actors Are Ineffective In Tackling Contemporary Global
Issues
On the other hand, it can be argued that NGOs and charitable foundations are ineffective in
tackling human rights, poverty and other contemporary global issues, as they often lack
transparency and accountability to the communities they claim to support.
In 2018, Oxfam faced a major scandal when it was revealed that aid workers had engaged in
sexual exploitation while working in Haiti after the 2010 earthquake. This incident not only
eroded public trust but also highlighted the lack of effective oversight mechanisms within
some of the largest NGOs.
Similarly, the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation has been criticised for its disproportionate
influence on global health policies, with some experts arguing that its focus on high-profile
diseases like polio diverts resources from broader systemic issues such as strengthening
local healthcare systems. This raises concerns about accountability and whether its
priorities truly reflect the needs of the communities it aims to serve.Evaluate The View That Non-State Actors Are Effective In Tackling Contemporary Global Issues. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan2
NGOs and charitable foundations also sometimes do more damage to the issues they seek to
tackle.
NGOs can inadvertently create dependency and reduce the power of local organisations
and populations rather than fostering sustainable solutions, particularly in fragile or conflict-
affected regions.
In South Sudan, the presence of international NGOs has created a parallel system of
governance, where reliance on foreign aid has undermined the development of local
institutions. This weakens the ability of local governments and communities to address
their own challenges, perpetuating cycles of poverty and dependence.
Oxfamʼs UK CEO Halima Begum recognised this in 2025, arguing that the traditional
model of international NGOs often do more damage than they do help, as they reinforce
dependency in the Global South by undermining the ability of countries to develop their
own support structures and strong local organisations.
Charitable foundations have also been criticised for prioritising short-term goals over long-
term capacity building, often focusing on donor-driven agendas rather than addressing
systemic local needs.
Further, in recent years the effectiveness of NGOs in tackling contemporary global issues has
been limited by government legislation, as states have used their sovereignty to restrict the
effectiveness of NGOs that they see as threatening their interests.
For example, in November 2024, the Venezuelan government passed a law widely referred
to as the Anti-NGO Law. The law requires all NGOs to obtain explicit state approval to
operate and imposes tight restrictions on their foreign funding, and public advocacy work.
As a result, prominent international NGOs including Acción Solidaria, which provided HIV
treatment and healthcare services to vulnerable communities, and Transparency
International, which worked on anti-corruption, were forced to drastically scale back their
operations in Venezuela.
Judgement
Overall, NGOs and charitable foundations are largely ineffective in tackling contemporary global
issues. While they can provide important short-term relief, in the long-term the fact they
unaccountable and often distanced from local populations means that they create dependency
and undermine the capacity of local actors to tackle poverty, human rights and other issues.
Paragraph Two ➡ Multinational Corporations
For: Non-State Actors Are Effective In Tackling Contemporary Global Issues
Multinational Corporations MNCs) are another non-state actor that hold significant power in
the global economy. It can be argued that they are effective in tackling poverty and
environmental damage in particular as they can use their economic clout to create jobs and
change their businesses to be renewable.
MNCs can influence poverty reduction through investment in local economies, and job creation,
particularly as many operate in some of the worldʼs poorest countries.
For example, Unilever operates extensive supply chains in rural areas of countries like
Kenya and India, sourcing raw materials from local farmers, which boosts incomes andEvaluate The View That Non-State Actors Are Effective In Tackling Contemporary Global Issues. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan3
supports small-scale agriculture.
Moreover, MNCs often invest in infrastructure and skills training as part of their operations,
which can have long-term benefits for local economies. For instance, Toyota‘s
manufacturing plants in South Africa provide vocational training programs that enhance the
skills of the local workforce, leading to better job prospects beyond the companyʼs
operations.
This demonstrates how MNCs can reduce poverty by fostering sustainable economic
development in some of the worldʼs poorest regions.
MNCsʼ corporate social responsibility initiatives can also play an effective role in improving
contemporary global issues, in particular as they are pressured to by other non-state actors
such as social movements.
Nestlé has developed its Nestlé Cocoa Plan and Nespresso AAA Sustainable Quality
Program, which focus on tackling poverty by improving the livelihoods of cocoa and coffee
farmers in countries like Ivory Coast, Ghana, and Colombia.
These initiatives aim to increase the productivity of small-scale farmers by providing
technical assistance and training in sustainable agricultural practices.
In response to the Fridays for Future protest movement in support of tackling climate
change, major corporations like Amazon, Microsoft, and Unilever publicly announced new
climate goals, such as achieving carbon neutrality or adopting green energy initiatives.
Against: Non-State Actors Are Ineffective In Tackling Contemporary Global
Issues
On the other hand, it can instead be argued that MNCs do significant harm to the environment
and increase poverty in the worldʼs poorest nations, as their primary goal is profit maximisation.
MNCs often exacerbate poverty through exploitative labour practices and limited reinvestment
in local economies, driven by the fact that their primary motive is short-term profit over
sustainable development.
For example, Foxconn, a major supplier for tech giants like Apple, has faced widespread
criticism for poor working conditions in its factories in countries like China, with workers
reporting excessive hours and low wages.
Furthermore, extractive industries, such as the mining industry operated by Glencore in the
Democratic Republic of Congo, often exploit local resources with minimal investment in the
local community. This simply exploits cheap labour and poor working conditions, before
leaving areas impoverished once operations cease.
Many MNCs also do major damage to the environment, including in the worldʼs most deprived
countries. Not only do oil and natural gas companies damage the environment through carbon
emissions, they also do so through oil spills.
For example, Royal Dutch Shell has been held accountable for extensive oil spills in the
Niger Delta, which have devastated local fishing and farming industries, destroying the
livelihoods of many communities.
Furthermore, it can be argued that CSR initiatives are insufficient and superficial attempts to
improve the reputation of MNCs to hide the systemic damage they do to the world around them.Evaluate The View That Non-State Actors Are Effective In Tackling Contemporary Global Issues. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan4
For example, while Nestlé has implemented programs like the Nestlé Cocoa Plan, it has also
faced criticism for child labour in its cocoa supply chains, highlighting inconsistencies
between public commitments and actual practices.
Additionally, greenwashing, where companies exaggerate their environmental efforts,
undermines trust in CSR programs. BP, for instance, rebranded as “Beyond Petroleumˮ but
continues to invest heavily in fossil fuels, illustrating how such initiatives can deflect
scrutiny without achieving meaningful change.
Judgement
Overall, multinational corporations do far more damage to the environment and to global
poverty than they help and are therefore ineffective in tackling contemporary global issues.
Though they do create jobs in developing states, these jobs often have very poor pay and
working conditions, whilst their CSR programmes are simply superficial attempts to cover their
significant environmental damage.
Paragraph Three ➡ Social Movements
For: Non-State Actors Are Effective In Tackling Contemporary Global Issues
Another important group of non-state actors that have grown in prominence in the past decade
in particular are international social movements. Cultural globalisation has made activists across
the world more connected than ever, which has enabled social movements to spread rapidly
across the world, putting significant pressure on governments and international institutions to
tackle issues such as climate change and poverty.
Social movements have plated an important role in mobilising for the protection of human
rights. A recent example of this is in 2024, when civil society mobilised in response to Israelʼs
military actions in Gaza.
Protests erupted globally, with millions demonstrating in cities across Europe, North
America, and the Middle East to denounce the high civilian death toll and call for immediate
humanitarian aid access. Social media played a critical role in spreading information and
coordinating demonstrations, with the #CeasefireNow hashtag trending globally.
These demonstrations have intensified public debate on the responsibility of governments
to respond to alleged human rights abuses and have led to growing calls for accountability,
with several governments facing pressure to reassess military aid policies and diplomatic
relations with Israel.
This shows how grassroots social movements across the world can play an important role in
protecting human rights, placing them at the forefront of public consciousness and
international discourse even if they are taking place in other parts of the world.
Similar can be seen in social movements to tackle climate change, in particular Greta
Thunbergʼs Fridays for Future movement, which mobilised and united millions of young people
worldwide, demanding urgent and ambitious climate policies.
Since Thunbergʼs first solo climate strike in 2018, Fridays for Future leveraged social media
and organised public demonstrations, inspiring over 14 million participants in more than
7,500 cities across 125 countries.Evaluate The View That Non-State Actors Are Effective In Tackling Contemporary Global Issues. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan5
This movement had an impact on policy in a number of countries, showing how cultural
globalisation and social media have allowed non-state actors who previously had little
voice/power to play a role in tackling contemporary global issues.
In Germany, the movement influenced the governmentʼs decision to set earlier deadlines
for coal phaseout and stricter emissions targets.
Similarly, in Sweden, Fridays for Future helped spark debates that led to stronger
commitments in the nationʼs Climate Policy Framework, which targets net-zero
emissions by 2045.
Against: Non-State Actors Are Ineffective In Tackling Contemporary Global
Issues
On the other hand, it can be argued that social movements are ineffective at tackling
contemporary global issues, as they are far from power and consistently unable to influence the
international community to pursue the often radical positions they advocate for.
Fridays For Future and other environmental social movements largely adopt a deep-green
ecology perspective, which suggests that to meaningfully tackle climate change capitalism
and the existing economic framework needs to be fundamentally restructured.
Despite this, the international community has continued to adopt a shallow-green
ecology perspective that seeks to reduce environmental damage whilst increasing
economic growth. This has been ineffective at tackling climate change, with global
temperatures set to rise by between 2.7°C and 3.1°C by the end of the century.
A clear example of the gap between social movementsʼ demands and actual policy
outcomes can be seen in the agreements at COP26 in 2021. Despite intense pressure
from environmental activists who called for more urgent action, the final agreements fell
short of the movementʼs key goals.
Activists had campaigned for a firm commitment to limit global temperature rise to
1.5°C and called for an immediate phase-out of coal, stronger financial
commitments for climate adaptation in developing countries, and a complete end to
fossil fuel subsidies.
However, the Glasgow Climate Pact only included a pledge to “phase downˮ rather
than “phase outˮ coal, and the financial aid for developing nations was far below the
requested $100 billion per year.
This demonstrates how international social movements are often ineffective in tackling
contemporary issues not only because they are far away from power, but also because their
aims often contradict statesʼ national economic interests.
Further, social movements have also been criticised as the involvement of celebrities can
sometimes oversimplify complex issues or result in “slacktivism,ˮ where public engagement is
limited to symbolic gestures like sharing posts on social media rather than driving substantial
change.
Moreover, celebrities can occasionally overshadow the voices of those most affected by
poverty and conflict, diverting attention from local activists and experts who may be more
deeply connected to the issues at hand.

Overall, social movements are arguably the non-state actor that is the most effective at tackling
contemporary global issues, as they connect activists across the world and put significant
public pressure on governments and international organisations.
Even they are largely ineffective, though, as they are far from political power and often have
radical aims that contradict the economic interests of states and multinational corporations,
which means that they are often ignored as a result.
Overall Argument
In conclusion, non-state actors are ineffective at tackling contemporary global issues in
different ways dependent on the particular actor.
Civil society and social movements are ineffective as they are far from power and their radical
aims are often ignored as they are contradictory to the economic interests of states.
Multinational corporations do active damage to the environment and poverty due to poor
working conditions and their prioritisation of profit.
NGOs and charitable foundations are unaccountable and largely distant from the local
populations they serve, which causes long-term damage to the capacity of local populations to
tackle issues such as poverty and environmental damage themselves.
