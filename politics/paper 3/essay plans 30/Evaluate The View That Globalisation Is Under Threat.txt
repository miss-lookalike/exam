Evaluate The View That Globalisation Is Under Threat.
Introduction
Key Definitions
Globalisation today is understood as the complex web of interconnectedness that defines global
politics across multiple dimensions, including the economic, political and cultural spheres. What
happens in one nation state influences and is influenced by decisions and factors across the world.

************************************************************************************************

Paragraph One ➡ The Rise Of Populism and Nationalism
For: Nationalism And Populism Have Grown, Threatening Globalisation and
International Cooperation
Perhaps the most important reason for the sense that globalisation is under threat is the rise of
nationalism and populism across Western democracies, which has disrupted global multilateral
cooperation and economic interdependence.
The election of Trump as US President in 2016 and 2024 is the most prominent example of
this. Trumpʼs campaigns capitalised on the anger of working-class Americans who felt left
behind by globalisation, particularly in regions where manufacturing jobs had been
outsourced to countries like China and Mexico due to free trade agreements such as
NAFTA.
His populist rhetoric, encapsulated in the slogan ‘America Firstʼ pushed back against
global institutions and multilateral trade deals, leading to policies aimed at protecting
American industries through tariffs and a reduction of US involvement in international
bodies such as the World Trade Organisation WTO and World Health Organisation
WHO.
During the COVID19 pandemic, Trump strongly criticised the WHO, accusing it of being
biased towards China and mishandling the early response to the virus. In 2020, he
announced the withdrawal of the US from the WHO, a move that undermined global
health cooperation at a critical time, as the US was the largest financial contributor to
the organisation.
This rise of populism was also highly significant across Europe, as seen in Brexit, the
dominance of Viktor Orbán in Hungary and the election of both right and left wing populists
in Italy. These movement argued that globalisation only benefitted elites and as a result
campaigned for greater border control and a reduced commitment to international
cooperation.
The UK leaving the European Union EU was a clear rejection of European political and
economic integration, with pro-Brexit campaigners arguing that the UK needed to
reclaim its sovereignty, especially over borders, laws, and trade policies.
These movements have not only pushed away from international cooperation, they have
also threatened and criticised many of the key beliefs and principles of globalisation and
multilateral cooperation, including free trade, economic integration and democracy.
This backlash against globalisation is in line with the beliefs of globalisation sceptics,
who believe that globalisation has exacerbated divisions between countries and that
nation states still retain sovereignty and will protect it through trade barriers and
immigration controls.
Furthermore, there has been growing resistance to cultural globalisation and its promotion of
American values and ideas in the Global South, which they see as undermining local traditions
and value.
The global spread of American cultural values through media, entertainment, and
consumerism has been met with significant backlash in various regions. In China, the
government has imposed strict controls on the internet and foreign cultural products,
limiting the influence of Western films, social media, and music.
Similarly, in India, there is increasing resistance to Western cultural influence, with some
groups pushing for the promotion of indigenous culture and values over what they perceive
as homogenisation and the encroachment of Western lifestyles .

************************************************************************************************

Against: There Has Been A Strong Liberal Pushback Against This
On the other hand, it can be argued that this rise of nationalism and populism doesnʼt pose a
major threat to globalisation, as there was still engagement with international institutions and
there has been a significant liberal pushback in the last few years.
In recent years, several key elections have reversed populist and nationalist policies,
reasserting globalisationʼs relevance and the commitment among left wing parties and their
voters to liberalism and international cooperation.
In Brazil, the 2022 election of Lula represented a decisive shift away from Jair Bolsonaroʼs
nationalist and isolationist policies. Lula pledged to restore Brazilʼs leadership in
international climate talks, particularly at COP27, and has reconnected Brazil with
multilateral environmental initiatives.
In the UK, although Brexit marked a nationalist departure from the EU, the election of Labour
in 2024 has led to greater engagement with Europe and a commitment to working together
on international issues, as shown in Starmerʼs visit to Brussels in early October 2024.
Further, even during the height of populist and nationalist rule, many of these governments
continued to engage with international institutions and cooperated with other countries,
demonstrating the enduring dominance of globalisation and suggesting that the extent to which
it was under threat shouldnʼt be overstated.
Despite Trumpʼs protectionist rhetoric, the US under his administration has not fully
withdrawn from multilateral organisations like the WTO and UN, and it remains an active
participant in NATO.
In Hungary, Prime Minister Viktor Orbán, while promoting nationalist policies and restricting
immigration, has not withdrawn from the European Union or its trade agreements,
demonstrating the pragmatic reality that even nationalist governments must operate within
the global system
These examples illustrate that while populists may challenge some aspects of globalisation,
they are largely unable or unwilling to disengage entirely from global economic and political
institutions.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, the more persuasive argument is that globalisation is somewhat under threat. As the
leader of the worldʼs most powerful country that dominates many international institutions,
Trumpʼs populism and nationalism in particular pose a significant threat to globalisation.
This being said, globalisation (and economic globalisation especially) is so entrenched that
populism and nationalism have currently only damaged it, rather than removing it
completely.

Paragraph Two ➡ Economic Protectionism
For: Economic Protectionism Is Becoming More and More Common
Another central argument that globalisation is under threat is the rise of economic protectionism
across the world, which undermines the principle of free trade that is central to globalisation
and the World Trade Organisation WTO in particular.
This is epitomised by the USChina trade war. Since 2018, the two largest economies in the
world have been engaged in a trade war, raising tariffs on hundreds of billions of dollarsʼ worth
of goods.
It has become even more disruptive in Trumpʼs second term, with Trump announcing a
145% tariff on Chinese imports in April 2025 (later reduced to 30%, whilst Trump also
implemented a 10% tariff on imports to the U.S. from any country.
This has significantly disrupted global supply chains and has shown that geopolitical
interests and competition are now being prioritised over a commitment to free trade and
shared prosperity.
This reflects a realist worldview because it highlights how states prioritise their national
interests and competition for power over cooperation, particularly when they come to
challenge each other for supremacy.
Both countries are now seeking to decouple their supply chains as part of a broader shift
toward self-reliance and isolationism.
In the United States, the Inflation Reduction Act 2022 offers substantial subsidies and
incentives for domestic manufacturing, particularly in green energy sectors, aiming to
reduce dependency on foreign-made goods, especially from China.
Meanwhile, Chinaʼs Made in China 2025 initiative is a national strategy aimed at
reducing reliance on foreign technology and promoting indigenous innovation,
positioning the country as a global leader in high-tech industries.
These moves signal a retreat from global economic integration, as both countries shift
toward economic nationalism and a focus on building independent supply chains.
This move towards economic protectionism is exacerbated by the growing disregard for WTO
rules, with more countries adopting unilateral trade measures that violate the organisationʼs
principles.
The WTOʼs dispute settlement mechanism has been increasingly sidelined, with the United
States, under both Trump and Biden, blocking appointments to the Appellate Body,
effectively paralysing the institutionʼs ability to enforce global trade rules.
This has undermined the credibility of the WTO as a multilateral arbiter of trade disputes,
encouraging countries to adopt protectionist policies with little fear of legal consequences.
For instance, the European Union and the United States have imposed tariffs on Chinese
goods, citing national security concerns and intellectual property theft, in direct
violation of WTO guidelines.
This erosion of WTO authority reflects a broader retreat from the rules-based international
trading system that underpinned decades of globalisation

************************************************************************************************

Against: Economic Cooperation and Interdependence Remains Very Strong
On the other hand, it can be argued that globalisation isnʼt really under threat as economic
cooperation and interdependence remain central to the global economy. The global economy
continues to be tightly interconnected through multinational corporations MNCs) and complex
global supply chains that are near impossible to untangle.
Major corporations like Apple, Toyota, and Amazon rely on a highly interconnected system
of production, sourcing components from multiple countries across continents. This global
interdependence is difficult to reverse, as the modern economy is built on the efficiencies
and cost advantages provided by global supply chains.
Even during the USChina trade war, American companies remain heavily reliant on Chinese
manufacturing for consumer goods and technology components, while Chinaʼs economy
continued to depend on access to the American market. This indicates that, despite political
rhetoric, the fundamental economic relationships that drive globalisation remain intact,
particularly in industries like technology, manufacturing, and finance.
Furthermore, trade agreements and regional partnerships intended to promote free trade
continue to thrive and facilitate economic cooperation and interdependence.
The Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership RCEP, which came into effect in
2022, is the worldʼs largest free trade agreement, encompassing 15 countries in Asia and
the Pacific, including China and Japan.
This agreement aims to reduce tariffs, promote trade, and enhance economic
integration among its members, showing that even amid protectionist rhetoric, many
countries are still pursuing globalised trade policies.
In May 2025, the UK signed important trade agreements with both India and the United
States to reduce tariffs and promote free trade. the agreement with India cut tariffs on 90%
of British goods exported to India and granted tariff-free access to 99% of Indian exports to
the UK.
These agreements suggest that globalisation isnʼt under threat, as major economies
continue to support reducing tariffs to support global trade as much as possible,
including the U.S. under Trump which is willing to sign agreements with other countries
to reduce tariffs.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, it is clear that the principle of free trade is under threat from the rise of economic
protectionism, however globalisation as a whole is not.
The interconnected nature of economies is so entrenched that it is near impossible to be
meaningfully threatened and the Chinese and American economies are still deeply dependent
and connected despite high tariffs.

Paragraph Three ➡ The Declining Influence Of International
Institutions
For: The Influence Of International Law and Institutions Has Been Undermined
A final important argument that globalisation is under threat is that, in the past decade
especially, the influence of international law and institutions has been weakened, therefore
undermining the framework of political globalisation.
In recent years, powerful states like China and the United States have increasingly rejected
international rulings and norms, eroding the authority of global governance bodies.
In 2016, China refused to accept a ruling by the Permanent Court of Arbitration that
invalidated its territorial claims in the South China Sea, continuing to assert control over
the region despite the tribunalʼs decision.
The United States has also resisted aspects of international law, particularly under
Donald Trump, who has withdrawn the U.S. from several multilateral agreements such
as the Paris Climate Accord and the Iran Nuclear Deal.
Moreover, the U.S. has not ratified the Rome Statute, which established the
International Criminal Court ICC, and neither have major powers like Russia and
China, meaning that many of the worldʼs most influential states are not subject to the
ICCʼs jurisdiction, which undermines international criminal law.
This reflects transformationalistsʼ views that globalisation is a transformative and highly
complex force that affects states unevenly. While it constrains weaker states, great
powers and superpowers are able to be less constrained.
Further, globalisation is arguably also under threat due to the division of the world into
competing geopolitical blocs and a more multipolar international order, which undermines
international cooperation to tackle pressing global issues and the ability of global institutions to
maintain peace and uphold human rights; a key goal of political globalisation.
The rise of revisionist powers like China, Russia, and Iran, who openly challenge the liberal
international order, has created significant divisions.
Russiaʼs invasion of Ukraine in 2022, which blatantly violated Ukraineʼs sovereignty, is a
stark example of how international law has failed to prevent conflict. Despite
widespread international condemnation and sanctions, Russia has continued its
aggression, with no resolution in sight. Its war has been supported by both China and
Iran.
This has severely undermined the credibility of the United Nations UN and the Security
Council UNSC in particular, which have been unable to enforce any meaningful action
to stop the war.
Similarly, the conflict between Israel and Hamas in Gaza has escalated in recent
years, with the UN and other international bodies unable to mediate a lasting
solution or prevent human rights violations.
These conflicts exemplify how political globalisation, particularly the institutions that are
supposed to uphold international law, are increasingly ineffective in maintaining global
stability and enforcing human rights.
The UNSC in particular is paralysed and ineffective due to the vetoes held by the
US, China and Russia in particular.

************************************************************************************************

Against: Effective Multilateral Cooperation Remains Despite These Challenges
On the other hand, it can be argued that political globalisation isnʼt in fact under threat, as
despite recent challenges, there remains effective multilateral cooperation on a range of
important issues that face individual countries and the world as a whole.
One of the clearest examples is the ongoing international efforts to combat climate change. The
Paris Climate Agreement, signed in 2015 and reaffirmed in subsequent climate summits such as
COP26 and COP27, continues to bring nearly 200 nations together in a collective effort to limit
global warming and reduce carbon emissions.
The agreementʼs success, with countries submitting Nationally Determined Contributions
NDCs) and working together on financing climate mitigation and adaptation, underscores
the strength of multilateral governance on issues that transcend national borders and pose
a significant challenge to the world as a whole.
This multilateral cooperation through international institutions could also be seen during the
COVID19 pandemic, when various IGOs were highly important in tackling the pandemicʼs
economic and health crises.
The COVAX initiative was led by the WHO to successfully distribute vaccines to low- and
middle-income countries, ensuring more equitable access to life-saving treatments despite
logistical and political challenges.
Further, the International Monetary Fund IMF and World Bank played a crucial role in
stabilising the global economy during the crisis, preventing the collapse of global financial
systems.
The IMF shifted away from strict conditionality toward providing rapid, emergency
financial support with fewer conditions. In 2020 alone, the IMF disbursed over $100
billion in emergency financing to more than 80 countries, focusing on health system
support and economic recovery.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, political globalisation and multilateral cooperation is the aspect of globalisation that is
most under threat. The worldʼs most powerful countries are increasingly willing and able to
ignore international rulings, which undermines international institutions and political
globalisation.
Political globalisation is also under threat from the rise of China in particular and how this is
creating a more divided and multipolar world. This will inevitably undermine international
cooperation.

************************************************************************************************
************************************************************************************************

Overall Argument
In conclusion, political globalisation is certainly under threat from the increasingly divided and
multipolar nature of global politics, but economic globalisation is not because it is so deeply
entrenched.
The rise of China and Russia, combined with the more aggressive stance towards China taken
on by America since Trumpʼs first election, has created a more multipolar and divided world.
This certainly poses a threat to the legitimacy of IGOs and the effectiveness of global
cooperation to tackle climate change and other pressing issues.
Whilst it does have economic effects and may somewhat undermine free trade, it ultimately
doesnʼt pose a major threat to economic globalisation. The interconnected nature of economies
is so entrenched that it is near impossible to be meaningfully threatened and even the Chinese
and American economies are still deeply dependent and connected despite economic
protectionism.
