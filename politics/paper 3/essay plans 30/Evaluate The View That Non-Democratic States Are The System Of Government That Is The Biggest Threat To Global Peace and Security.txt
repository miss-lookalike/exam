Evaluate The View That Non-Democratic States Are The System Of Government That Is The Biggest Threat To Global Peace and Security.

Introduction
Key Definitions
In this essay, ‘rogue statesʼ are included in ‘non-democratic statesʼ, whilst ‘semi-democratic
statesʼ and ‘failed statesʼ are not.

************************************************************************************************
************************************************************************************************

Paragraph One ➡ Non-Democratic States vs Failed States
For: Non-Democratic States And Their Collapse Leads To Political Violence and
Instability
Non-democratic states are political systems where the power lies in the hands of a single
leader, a ruling elite, the military or a political party, with little to no input from the general
population.
There is a strong argument to suggest that non-democratic states are the biggest threat to
global peace and security, as many of them face domestic instability due to repression and lack
of political participation, which can lead to uprisings and civil wars that can spill over and cause
global instability.
For example, the Arab Spring of 2011 demonstrated how popular discontent with autocratic
regimes could explode into widespread revolts and eventually civil wars, with deadly civil
wars in Syria against Bashar Al Assadʼs regime and in Libya against Muammar Gaddafiʼs
autocratic regime.
The downfall of autocratic regimes also often leads to power vacuums, resulting in further
instability and conflict that can also spill over into different countries and disrupt the global
order. The lack of legal avenues for change means that political transitions often occur through
force, leading to protracted conflict.
The fall of Gaddafiʼs regime in Libya in 2011 led to a power vacuum, sparking internal
conflict among rival factions that continues to destabilise the region. This instability has
fueled arms trafficking and migrant flows across North Africa and Europe, affecting
neighbouring countries and challenging global security.
The ousting of President Mobutu in the Democratic Republic of Congo in 1997 created a
power struggle that led to years of civil war involving multiple regional actors.
This conflict has drawn in neighbouring countries and contributed to prolonged
instability, impacting Central Africaʼs security and economic development.
The war has become known as Africaʼs World War, involving Rwanda, Uganda, and
Angola, and resulting in over 5 million deaths and the displacement of millions more,
severely hampering regional growth and stability.

************************************************************************************************

Against: Failed States Cause Greater Instability and Regional Conflict
On the other hand, it can be argued that non-democratic/autocratic states are not the biggest
threat to global peace and security, as failed states cause far more spillover into different
countries and create much greater regional and global instability.
A failed state is a country where the government has lost its ability to perform basic
functions necessary for maintaining law and order, protecting its citizens, and providing
essential services. In such states, institutions are weak or non-existent, leading to a
collapse of governance.
They pose a major threat to global peace and security, as they precipitate the complete
collapse of order and their economies, which creates the conditions for chronic violence and
exploitation by self-interested actors.
These militant and terrorist actors engage in arms trafficking and profiteering which can
destabilise entire regions. The absence of a functioning government allows these groups to
operate with impunity, recruiting fighters, smuggling weapons, and launching attacks both
domestically and internationally.
After the collapse of government in 1991, power in Somalia was fragmented among militias,
warlords and terrorist groups. The absence of a stable government has allowed piracy to
flourish off the Somali coast, disrupting international shipping routes and prompting military
responses the US and UK.
It also led to terrorist groups al-Shabaab and ISIS exploiting the lack of central authority
and expanding their influence, which then also enabled them to grow in power in other
parts of Africa.
Failed states also threaten global security, as neighbouring states are frequently drawn into the
conflicts, as they are forced to manage refugee flows and the consequences of insecurity along
their borders.
The outbreak of conflict in Sudan in 2023 has driven over a million refugees into
neighbouring countries such as Chad, South Sudan, and Egypt, escalating regional
instability.
In Chad, the sudden influx of Sudanese refugees has intensified local conflicts over
scarce resources like water and grazing land, with clashes erupting between refugees
and local communities.
South Sudan, already grappling with its own internal conflicts, has seen heightened
security concerns along its northern border, where armed groups from Sudan have
crossed over, threatening local populations and further destabilising the region.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, the more persuasive argument is that non-democratic states are a greater threat to
global peace and stability than failed states are. Though both cause regional and global
instability, non-democratic states are a greater threat as it is often the collapse of autocratic
states that leads to failed states, whilst democracies are far more likely to be stable.

************************************************************************************************
************************************************************************************************

Paragraph Two ➡ Rogue States vs Democratic States
For: Rogue States Intentionally Disrupt Global Peace and Stability
Perhaps the most persuasive argument that non-democratic states are the system of
government that is the biggest threat to global peace and security, is that a number of them are
‘rogue statesʼ which disregard the rule of law and intentionally seek to disrupt the international
order, leading to instability.
The term rogue state was popularised by the United States in the 1990s to describe nations
that were seen as threats to global peace and security due to their defiance of international
norms.
These states typically engage in aggressive or unpredictable behaviour, such as pursuing
weapons of mass destruction, supporting terrorism, or violating international treaties. This
can significantly threaten other states and therefore disrupt global security.
There has been a growth in their power over the past decade, challenging US
hegemony and the stability it created due to no state being able or willing to challenge
its power.
Russiaʼs actions on the global stage have significantly undermined regional and global stability.
It has intentionally sought to do so and therefore undermine American dominance of the
international system.
Not only has Russia created significant instability in Europe through its invasion of Ukraine
in 2022, it has also done so in Europe through its strong military and financial support for
Bashar al-Assadʼs regime in Syria which ensured he won the Syrian Civil War and that it
continued for nearly a decade
Similarly, Iran has caused great instability and disruption to global peace through its nuclear
programme and support for militant groups like Hezbollah, Hamas and the Houthis.
Iranʼs support for these militant groups has caused instability and violence across the
Middle East, including prolonging the civil war in Yemen and instability in both Lebanon and
Israel. Its support of Hezbollah and Hamas allows it to pursue a central objective of
destabilising and threatening Israel.
Its attempt to develop its own nuclear weapons programme has also caused instability, as
Israel and the United States in particular have used air strikes and economic sanctions to
prevent it from gaining Weapons of Mass Destruction WMDs).
By contrast, it can be argued that democratic states cause far less instability and war, as power
is decentralised and they strongly believe in the protection of human rights and liberties.
‘Democratic peace theoryʼ described the fact that democracies tend to avoid war with one
another, due to diplomacy and economic cooperation. Between 1950 and 1990, democratic
states were involved in only 12% of all international conflicts, despite representing a
significant proportion of the global population.
It can also be argued that democratic states promote global peace and security by using
their military power to promote human rights and democracy across the world.
For example, the NATO-led intervention in Kosovo in 1999 helped stop ethnic cleansing
and eventually led to Kosovoʼs path towards democracy.

************************************************************************************************

Against: Democratic States Have Caused Greater Instability In The Name Of
Promoting Democracy
On the other hand, it can be argued that democratic states are in fact a greater threat to global
peace an security, as their actions in the name of human rights and democracy have in fact
frequently caused war and further in stability.
The US and UK-led interventions in Afghanistan 2001 and Iraq 2003 were justified, in
part, on the grounds of promoting democracy and protecting human rights, but had little
success in doing so and caused mass casualties and regional instability.
In Iraq, while Saddam Husseinʼs dictatorship was overthrown, the country experienced
years of instability, sectarian violence, and the rise of extremist groups like ISIS since the
US and UK-invasion which itself killed an estimated 200,000 Iraqi citizens. The intention to
impose democracy failed and created a power vacuum that led to great instability.
Similarly, in Afghanistan while initial progress was made with elections held in the 2000s,
the Talibanʼs return to power in 2021 following the US withdrawal shows the failed attempt
to impose democracy which came at a significant cost.
Further, it must be recognised that whilst the United States talks about the promotion of human
rights, it is very happy to support states that cause great suffering and instability if it is
conducted by its allies/in its geopolitical interests.
For example, it has continued to send arms to Israel throughout the Israel-Gaza war despite
the deaths of over 40,000 civilians and widespread reported human rights abuses.
It can be argued that non-democratic states in fact pose a much smaller threat to global peace
and instability, as China in particular strongly promotes non-intervention and protecting the
sovereignty of states.
Whilst this does mean that human rights abuses within countries arenʼt addressed, it
doesnʼt disrupt the regional balance of power the unintended consequences that military
intervention often does.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, there is no doubt that the United States in particular caused major instability and war in
the Middle East in the name of democracy and that both democratic and non-democratic states
can cause instability and war. However, currently rogue non-democratic states such as Russia
and Iran pose a far greater threat, as they intentionally seek to destabilise global peace and the
US-led liberal international order.

************************************************************************************************
************************************************************************************************

Paragraph Three ➡ Non-Democratic States vs Semi-Democratic
States
Against: Semi-Democratic States Cause Disruption Within The Existing
International Order
A final argument that non-democratic states arenʼt the biggest threat to global peace and
stability is that semi-democratic states are posing an increasing risk, as leaders pursue their
interests whilst hiding behind a democratic facade.
Semi-democratic states are political systems that exhibit both democratic and autocratic
features.
While these states may hold regular elections and have some degree of political pluralism,
their democratic institutions are often weakened by a lack of transparency, manipulation of
electoral processes, or restrictions on civil liberties.
Semi-democratic institutions threaten global peace and security, as they are often members of
IGOs and supranational institutions and receive the benefits, yet weaken these institutions as
they donʼt truly respect their principles and are willing to cooperate with autocratic states,
therefore undermining the aims of democratic, liberal states from within the system.
Under President Erdoğan, Turkey has undergone increasing authoritarianism, with Erdoğan
using electoral manipulation, media censorship and suppression of political opponents to
maintain power. This is despite the fact it has long been seen as a key democratic ally of the
West in the region.
This semi-democratic rise in a country that is highly important geopolitically, due to its
position in both the Mediteranean and Middle East, has threatened global peace as it has
been willing to build ties with Russia and other non-democratic states.
This has undermined the unity of Western, democratic countries against rising
authoritarianism and therefore threatened the balance of power and global stability.
Similarly, under Prime Minister Viktor Orbán, Hungary has shifted from being a fully democratic
state to a semi-democratic state. While Hungary holds regular elections and retains many
democratic institutions, Orbánʼs government has systematically undermined judicial
independence, restricted media freedom, and curtailed the power of civil society organisations.
This has threatened global stability and peace as it has created tensions within the
European Union and undermined its ability to promote and protect democracy across the
world.
Whilst the EU has taken steps to address democratic backsliding in Hungary, it is still a
member of the EU and therefore harms its global reputation.

************************************************************************************************

For: Non-Democratic States Intentionally Disrupt and Destabilise Democracies
On the other hand, it can be argued that non-democratic states are a far greater threat to global
peace and stability, as they intentionally destabilise democracies in order to cause instability
and challenge the global balance of power.
They therefore damage cooperation by liberal countries globally by encouraging democratic
backsliding in Western countries and supporting semi-democratic governments.
Russian interference in the 2016 US presidential election is a key example of this, as Russia
made a concerted effort to destabilise American democracy by spreading disinformation and
amplifying political divisions.
Through coordinated social media campaigns, Russian operatives promoted polarising
content, intending to erode public trust in democratic institutions and influence voter
sentiment.
This interference not only undermined U.S. political stability but also highlighted Russiaʼs
strategy of weakening liberal democracies to challenge Western unity and global influence.
This shows how non-democratic states have played an important role in seeking to create
semi-democratic states.
China has also used semi-democratic states as a way to gain economic leverage and
destabilise the West. For example, it has collaborated with Hungary, which has become an
important supporter of its influence within the European Union, therefore causing rifts in a key
competitor.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, semi-democratic states do pose a threat to global peace and stability from within liberal
IGOs and alliances. However, non-democratic states pose a greater threat as they uphold these
regimes and intentionally seek to cause disruption in liberal alliances and institutions.

************************************************************************************************
************************************************************************************************

Overall Argument
In conclusion, it is clear that non-democratic institutions are the system of government that is
the biggest threat to global peace and stability.
In the current world order, rogue states Russia and Iran in particular cause the most disruption
and war by intentionally seeking to undermine the United Statesʼ global hegemony. They do so
by proposing up other non-democratic regimes and terrorist organisations, whilst also using
their own hard power.
Further, they are often unstable and their collapse can lead fo power vacuums that create civil
wars and consequent regional instability.
