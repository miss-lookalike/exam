Paragraph One ➡ The Economy
For: The EU Has Been A Success For EU Member States
On the one hand, it can be argued that the EU has been an economic success for its member
states, as its huge single market and customs union has enabled it to negotiate favourable trade
deals and improve the economies of its member states.
The European Unionʼs single market, representing over 15% of global GDP, enables the EU to
secure favourable trade agreements that support economic growth across its member states.
Acting as a unified bloc, the EU negotiates on behalf of its 27 members, which collectively
allows it to secure trade deals that smaller or medium-sized states would struggle to
achieve alone. As it is collectively the largest trading partner for over 80 countries, it has
much greater leverage.Evaluate The View That The EU Has Been A Success For Its Member States. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan1
For instance, the EUCanada Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement CETA
eliminated tariffs on 98% of goods traded between the two regions, benefiting EU
exporters and reducing costs for businesses.
Similarly, the EUSouth Korea Free Trade Agreement has increased EU exports to South
Korea by 76% since its implementation, benefiting member states with strong export
sectors, such as Germany in machinery and Ireland in pharmaceuticals.
These agreements bolster GDP and support jobs, with estimates indicating that over 36
million jobs in the EU are directly dependent on exports to non-EU countries.
For countries like Portugal and the Czech Republic, which rely on export markets for
economic growth, these trade agreements provide critical economic opportunities,
showcasing the success of the EUʼs single market in supporting a wide range of
member state economies.
Further, the customs union, which eliminates internal tariffs, also provides seamless access for
EU businesses to a market of over 450 million consumers, further strengthening member
statesʼ economies and giving them the opportunity for significant economic growth.
For example, since joining the EU in 2004, Polandʼs GDP per capita has risen from around
€5,000 at the time of accession to over €20,000 in 2024. This economic improvement is
attributed to access to the single market and customs union for Polandʼ impressive
manufacturing and industrial sector, which have helped Poland become one of the fastest-
growing economies in the EU.
Against: The EU Hasnʼt Been A Success For EU Member States
On the other hand, it can be argued that the EU hasnʼt been an economic success for its
member states as its supranational structure limits member statesʼ ability to protect their own
economies, as they are subject to EU-wide policies and trade agreements that may not align
with individual economic priorities.
By pooling authority over trade and competition policies, EU member states sacrifice a
degree of economic sovereignty, leaving them unable to fully control tariffs, subsidies, or
other protections for domestic industries.
For example, under EU competition law, countries are generally prohibited from
providing state aid to industries that may need support, such as steel or agriculture,
without prior approval from the European Commission.
This restriction can hurt countries with struggling sectors, such as Italy which has
struggling automotive and steel industries and may have wished to support them in the
context of global competition to aid their competitiveness and prevent job losses. However,
they are unable to due to EU policies.
In particular, it can be argued that the EU hasnʼt been a success for the EUʼs poorer states, as
the economic benefits within the EU are unevenly distributed, often favouring wealthier member
states over poorer ones.
Wealthier countries like Germany and the Netherlands benefit greatly from the EUʼs single
market and customs union, using their strong export industries to tap into vast EU markets
without barriers. Germany, for example, exports over 50% of its goods to other EU
countries, taking full advantage of the customs-free single market.Evaluate The View That The EU Has Been A Success For Its Member States. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan2
In contrast, less affluent member states, particularly in Southern and Eastern Europe, often
struggle to compete within the single market.
In 2022, the unemployment rate in Greece and Spain was 12.6% and 13.3% respectively,
compared to the EU average of 6%, illustrating the persistent economic challenges
faced by some Southern European economies within the single market.
The eurozone crisis highlighted these inequalities, exposing structural flaws in the euro that
made it difficult for economically weaker nations, such as Greece, Spain, and Portugal, to
recover.
Greece, in particular, faced stringent austerity measures imposed by the EU and the
International Monetary Fund IMF as a condition for bailout packages, leading to long-
term economic hardship, including a 25% decrease in GDP and youth unemployment
rates exceeding 50% at the height of the crisis.
At the same time, countries like Germany emerged from the crisis relatively unscathed,
poorer economies endured years of economic stagnation, high unemployment, and
reduced public services.
Judgement
Overall, there is no doubt that the EU has benefitted certain wealthier states more than it has
poorer states, however for the vast majority of its member states it has been an economic
success.
Tariff-free access to the EUʼs internal market has given member states a major economic
opportunity, whilst the EUʼs collective economic weight has enabled it to negotiate trade deal
terms with other countries that are far better than they would have been able to negotiate on
their own.
Paragraph Two ➡ Geopolitics/Security
For: The EU Has Been A Success For EU Member States
Though not as important as the EUʼs economic influence on member states, it can also be
argued that the EU has been a success for member states as it has enhanced their geopolitical
influence. The EU positions itself as a global leader in promoting democracy, human rights, and
sustainable development and has significant soft power as a result.
The EU and its member states collectively account for over 50% of global development aid,
providing approximately €80 billion annually. This assistance is strategically allocated to
support economic development, poverty alleviation, and governance reforms, especially in
Africa, the Middle East, and the EUʼs eastern neighbourhood.
For example, through the European Development Fund EDF, the EU has provided
billions of euros to Sub-Saharan Africa to improve education, healthcare, and
infrastructure, often tying aid to commitments on human rights and good governance.
As a result of these efforts, the EU has a strong global reputation as the worldʼs champion of
liberal values such as democracy and human rights, particularly as the US looks likely to
once again step back from doing so under a second Trump presidency. This gives member
states with a shared commitment to liberal values major influence over other countries,
which they wouldnʼt have on their own.Evaluate The View That The EU Has Been A Success For Its Member States. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan3
For example, after the 2013 Rana Plaza disaster, the EU pushed for labour reform by
threatening to remove Bangladeshʼs preferential trade access under the “Everything but
Armsˮ program if safety standards werenʼt improved.
This led to significant improvements in factory conditions and workplace safety through
the creation of the Accord on Fire and Building Safety, positively affecting millions of
workers.
Further, the EU is increasingly coordinating its foreign and security policies in order to improve
its member statesʼ security. This is especially driven by French President Macron, who has long
argued that the EU needs to “avoid being followersˮ and build an independent geopolitical
strategy separate from the US.
The EUʼs response to Russiaʼs 2022 invasion of Ukraine, for example, was very strong. It
imposed multiple rounds of sanctions targeting Russiaʼs financial system, energy exports,
and key industries, cutting off critical funding sources for Moscow.
This economic pressure combined with EU support for Ukraine in the form of military
aid and humanitarian assistance, has had a significant positive impact on Ukraineʼs
chances of winning the war.
This once again demonstrates how cooperation within the EU enables member states to
have greater overall geopolitical and military influence than they would alone.
Against: The EU Hasnʼt Been A Success For EU Member States
On the other hand, it can be argued that the EU hasnʼt been a geopolitical/military success for
member states as it has been unable to compete with the China and the United States,
therefore leaving EU member states left behind and at a disadvantage.
Unlike the United States, which has the worldʼs most powerful military, or China, which is
expanding its military presence globally, the EU lacks the independent military strength to
protect itself or project its interests beyond its borders.
The EUʼs lack of a unified, centralised military force means that it is very dependent on
NATO and the United States in particular to have global influence and to respond to
major security crises.
For example, in the 1990s, during the wars in Bosnia and Kosovo, the EU was unable
to mobilise a unified military response to prevent ethnic violence and humanitarian
atrocities. Instead, NATO, heavily led by US forces, intervened with operations like
Operation Deliberate Force and Operation Allied Force to stabilise the region.
More recently, with the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, the EU has played a
supportive role by imposing sanctions and providing economic and humanitarian
aid, but it was kept out of peace negotiations between the U.S., Russia and Ukraine
and remains very dependent on the U.S. to offer military guarantees.
A central reason for the EUʼs inability to project influence geopolitically is its internal divisions
over foreign policy, which prevent it from taking a strong, unified stance and weaken its
geopolitical impact. This demonstrates that the EU may be a successful military alliance, but it
struggles to be a security alliance.
Member states have diverse interests and priorities in foreign policy, which can lead to stark
disagreements on how to approach global issues.Evaluate The View That The EU Has Been A Success For Its Member States. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan4
For example, during the US-led War on Terror, EU member states took opposing
stances: the UK and Poland joined the US-led coalition in Iraq, while Germany and
France strongly opposed the intervention.
More recently, this has undermined efforts to find an effect way to project European power
in the context of the USChina rivalry, with disagreement on whether to align more closely
with Washington or pursue a more balanced stance.
France and Germany have expressed concerns about becoming overly dependent on
the US, with French President Macron calling for “strategic autonomy.ˮ However,
Eastern European countries, particularly those bordering Russia, generally support
closer alignment with the US, fearing Russian aggression.
This indecision and the EUʼs lack of hard power have led to its member states being
overlooked in key strategic decisions and alliances, such as in Asia-Pacific security
arrangements.
Judgement
Overall, the EU hasnʼt been as much as a success in making itself a major geopolitical actor as
many predicted and pursued, with the bloc struggling to compete with the United States and
China in the current geopolitical world.
This being said, it hasnʼt been a major failure for EU member states, as it is still able to have
greater influence than member states would have been able to have alone, particularly due to
its soft power.
Paragraph Three ➡ Structural Influence
For: The EU Has Been A Success For EU Member States
Finally, it can be argued that the EU has been a success for its member states, as it has given
them significant structural power in global trade and in international governmental organisations
IGOs).
The EUʼs structural power in trade and regulation makes it one of the most influential players in
the global economy, where it often acts as a “regulatory superpower.ˮ As the worldʼs largest
single market, the EU leverages its economic size to set global standards in areas such as
environmental protection, consumer safety, and data privacy.
This phenomenon, often referred to as the “Brussels Effect,ˮ occurs when multinational
companies and even other countries adapt their practices to comply with EU regulations to
access its market of over 450 million consumers.
A prime example is the EUʼs General Data Protection Regulation GDPR, which has set
an international standard for data privacy. Countries including Japan, Brazil, and South
Korea have adopted similar data privacy laws in response, illustrating the EUʼs far-
reaching regulatory influence.
Additionally, the EU plays a central role in the World Trade Organization WTO, where its
collective weight allows it to negotiate and advocate for fair trade practices and high
standards.
In the WTO, the EU led efforts to ban hormone-treated beef, successfully advocating for
stricter food safety standards that influenced global agricultural practices.Evaluate The View That The EU Has Been A Success For Its Member States. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan5
Without being a member of the EU, the vast majority of member states would be in a
position where they had little influence over global trade rules and had to accept them,
instead of being able to shape them as they can in the EU.
The EU also gives member states structural power through active participation in major IGOs,
such as the G7, G20, International Monetary Fund IMF, and World Health Organisation WHO.
Within these institutions, the EU uses its economic clout to influence policies on issues
ranging from financial stability to global health and climate action.
For example, the EU played a critical role in shaping the Paris Climate Accord by
pushing for binding commitments to limit global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius, setting
an ambitious precedent for other countries and upholding its importance even when the
US pulled out under Donald Trump.
As a prominent G7 and G20 member, the EU represents its collective economic interests,
working alongside other global powers to shape responses to crises such as the COVID19
pandemic and the 2008 financial crisis.
Against: The EU Hasnʼt Been A Success For EU Member States
Whilst being a member of the EU may give member states greater structural influence in trade
and IGOs, it can be argued that the EU hasnʼt been a success because it has decreased each
individual member stateʼs control over the decisions that affect their country.
The Treaty of Rome in 1957 included a commitment to an “ever closer unionˮ and over time this
has been realised in deeper integration, with supranational institutions such as the European
Commission and European Central Bank ECB becoming more important and member states
losing their ability to veto decisions in more and more policy areas.
As a consequence, the sovereignty of member states has been eroded, which can be
argued to be a huge failure for member states, which can no longer independently
determine their future.
The commitment to an “ever closer unionˮ can be seen in the increasing use of Qualified
Majority Voting since the EU was founded, which undermines member statesʼ sovereignty as
they can be forced to accept decisions they disagree with.
QMV enables EU legislation to be passed by a ‘double majorityʼ in the Council of the
European Union rather than requiring unanimity, which means that countries can be
outvoted and cannot veto critical policies affecting their national interests.
A double majority requires 55% of member states to vote in favour, whilst these
member states must represent at least 65% of the EU population.
Over the years, QMV has been extended to include areas like immigration, trade, and
climate policy, resulting in more decisions where individual states cannot exercise veto
power. The Lisbon Treaty 2009 expanded QMV to over 40 new areas, including judicial
cooperation and energy policy, further reducing member statesʼ sovereignty over important
policy domains and forcing states to accept decisions they disagree with.
In 2015, under QMV, the EU approved mandatory migrant relocation quotas, requiring
countries like Hungary, Poland, and the Czech Republic to accept a share of refugees,
despite their strong opposition.Evaluate The View That The EU Has Been A Success For Its Member States. 30 Politics Explained Essay Plan6
Poland was again overruled in 2020 when the EU set more ambitious climate targets
under QMV, mandating a reduction in carbon emissions by at least 55% by 2030,
despite Polandʼs concerns about the impact on its coal-dependent economy.
Judgement
Overall, the EU has been a success for member states by giving them greater structural
influence. Though the influence the EU has does come at the expense of some of its member
statesʼ sovereignty, this is ultimately a worthwhile tradeoff and member states can always leave
the EU to regain their sovereignty as Brexit demonstrated.
Overall Argument
In conclusion, EU membership has been a success for its member states, in particular due to
the economic benefits it brings.
The EUʼs geopolitical and military influence hasnʼt lived up to the ambitious visions of some
within the alliance, as it is struggling to project its power in the context of USChina competition
and relies on the United States for its security.
However, this hasnʼt harmed member states who would be in an even more precarious position
alone and who benefit massively from the EU economically.
In particular, the 450 million person internal market the EU provides and its ability to
negotiate favourable trade terms with other countries has significantly boosted the
economic growth of its member states, which would have grown much more slowly without
EU membership.
