Evaluate The View That Economic Global Governance Is Ineffective. (30)

Introduction
Key Definitions
To analyse whether economic global governance is effective, this essay will look at five central
international governmental organisations IGOs) that form economic global governance; the
International Monetary Fund IMF, The World Bank, the World Trade Organisation WTO, the
G7 and the G20.

************************************************************************************************

Paragraph One ➡ The International Monetary Fund (IMF) and
World Bank
Against: Economic Global Governance Is Effective

Both the IMF and World Bank were established at the Bretton Woods Conference in 1944. The
IMFʼs central aim is to provide financial assistance to help countries struggling economically
and to ensure global trade and economic stability. The World Bankʼs key focus is on reducing
poverty and promoting countriesʼ long-term development. The two IGOs often work together
and have a similar neoliberal ethos.
One important argument that the IMF and World Bank are effective is that they play an important
role in tackling global financial crises.
One of the IMFʼs key strengths is its ability to provide rapid financial assistance to countries
in economic distress, helping them stabilise their economies and avoid default. This role as
a lender of last resort is particularly important during global or regional financial crises and
maintains global economic stability.
For example, in April 2025 the IMF approved a 48-month bailout package for Argentina,
in order to stabilise the Argentinian economy that was at risk of financial crisis, due to
limited foreign currency reserves. The IMFʼs ability to bolster the countryʼs foreign
exchange reserves helped to prevent an economic crisis that could have had significant
regional and global consequences.
Though the World Bank focuses less on immediate crises, it does sometimes play a role and
is also important in tackling global crises such as climate change.
In response to the rising global food and energy crisis resulting from the Ukraine-Russia
war in 2022, the World Bank mobilised more than $30 billion to support countries
impacted by rising prices.
In Egypt, the support helped the government address food insecurity by stabilising
the grain supply through targeted subsidies and imports.
In 2020, the Bank committed more than $20 billion to climate-related projects,
supporting renewable energy initiatives, green infrastructure, and programs designed to
build climate resilience in vulnerable countries.
The Noor Solar Complex in Morocco, backed by World Bank financing, is one of the
largest solar power plants in the world and has helped Morocco reduce its reliance
on fossil fuels.
It can also be argued that the IMF and World Bank are effective as they help countries improve
their economic systems and tackle poverty.
Alongside lending, the IMF provides developing countries in particular with technical
expertise and training in areas such as tax policy, monetary policy and fiscal policy to
improve their capacity.
In the early 2000s, Cambodia sought to modernise its central banking and financial
systems. The IMF provided technical assistance to improve Cambodiaʼs monetary policy
framework and banking regulations.
By enhancing the capabilities of the National Bank of Cambodia and providing training
to local officials, the IMF helped the country stabilise its banking sector, manage
inflation more effectively, and sustain its economic growth.
Since it began, the World Bank has provided over $160 billion in concessional financing to
the worldʼs poorest countries, helping them build essential infrastructure, improve
healthcare, and expand educational opportunities. This financing has helped lift millions out
of poverty and promote long-term growth.
In Ethiopia, for example, World Bank funding has supported healthcare, infrastructure,
and agricultural reforms, contributing to sustained economic growth rates of above 8%
annually in the 2010s.
The Bank has helped finance critical roads, clean water projects, and agriculture-
focused investments that have increased food security and bolstered long-term growth.

************************************************************************************************

For: Economic Global Governance Is Ineffective
On the other hand, it can be argued that the IMF and World Bank are in fact ineffective and do
major harm to the global economy and the developing countries they claim to help, due to the
flawed neoliberal assumptions that guide their advice and lending practices.
This reflects the fact that both the IMF and World Bank are structured in a way that
disproportionately favours wealthy, Western nations. Voting power both the World Bank and
IMF is determined by financial contributions, meaning that richer countries have
significantly more influence over decisions than poor countries, the primary recipients of
their loans.
The U.S. is the only country with veto power over structural changes within the World
Bank and holds around 16.5% of the total voting power in the IMF, which, combined with
the requirement that major decisions need an 85% majority, effectively grants the U.S. a
de facto veto over key IMF policies.
During the 1980s and 1990s in particular, the IMF and World Bank supported structural
adjustment programmes SAPs), which made loans to countries in need conditional on these
countries implementing austerity measures, including cutting public spending, privatising state-
owned enterprises, and reducing social services. This had disastrous consequences,
demonstrating the flawed assumptions behind both IGOsʼ neoliberal thought.
In the 1990s, Zambia faced an economic crisis due to high external debt and dependence
on copper exports. The IMF and World Bank made lending conditional upon implementing
an SAP that included widespread privatisation, deregulation, and cuts in public spending on
healthcare and education.
This resulted in mass layoffs, especially from state-owned enterprises, driving poverty
levels from 49% in 1989 to over 80% by the mid-1990s. The reforms did little to help the
economy, which only began to recover 10 years later.
This can also be seen more recently in the case of Greece in 2011, where IMF-backed
austerity measures during the Eurozone debt crisis led to 27% unemployment, massive cuts
in pensions and healthcare, and widespread protests.
Furthermore, by providing loans, even at low-interest rates, the Bank contributes to the
growing debt burden of developing nations. Many countries find themselves trapped in
cycles of borrowing, struggling to repay loans while simultaneously needing to take on more
debt to fund basic services.
This has been particularly problematic in Africa and Latin America, where countries
such as Kenya and Argentina have been forced to prioritise debt repayments over social
spending, limiting their ability to invest in public services like healthcare, education, and
infrastructure.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, the World Bank and IMF arenʼt effective. Having financial assistance in place for
countries when they need it is certainly important, however the condition of austerity attached
to these loans has had disastrous effects on many countries; furthering poverty and suffering.
Until the IMF and World Bank remove these conditions and the neoliberal beliefs present in their
organisations, then they will not be effective.

Paragraph Two ➡ The World Trade Organisation (WTO)
Against: Economic Global Governance Is Effective
The WTOʼs primary aim is to promote free trade and prevent against protectionism, ensuring
that global trade flows as smoothly, predictably, and freely as possible. It achieves this by
providing a forum for negotiating trade agreements and settling disputes between members.
A key argument that the WTO is effective is that is has successfully promoted free trade and
therefore driven economic growth since its founding 1995. It has done so by facilitating
multilateral trade agreements that have helped open markets and boost global trade.
The Bali Trade Facilitation Agreement 2013, for example successfully reduced red tape
and simplified customs procedures globally. It is estimated to reduce global trade costs by
14.3% on average, helping small businesses and landlocked countries participate more
effectively in international trade.
In the decades following the establishment of the WTO, the volume of world merchandise
trade has more than tripled, growing from $5 trillion in 1995 to over $22 trillion in 2021.
The WTO can also be argued to be effective as it plays an important role in resolving disputes
and prevents trade wars as a result. By enforcing agreed-upon rules and offering a platform for
legal resolution, the WTO has been instrumental in maintaining global trade stability and
protecting the interests of smaller economies that may not have the power to negotiate on their
own.
The WTOʼs Dispute Settlement Body DSB plays a critical role in managing trade conflicts
between member states, offering a neutral and legally-binding process for resolving
disputes.
This prevents countries from engaging in unilateral retaliatory actions, which could
escalate into broader trade conflicts or even trade wars.
This can be seen in the dispute between Brazil and The United States over cotton subsidies
between 2002 and 2009, when Brazil argued that the United States provided illegal
subsidies to its cotton farmers, violating WTO agreements and distorting global cotton
prices, harming Brazilian farmers.
The DSB ruled in favour of Brazil in 2004, and after appeals, the WTO confirmed that
the U.S. had violated international trade rules. The U.S. agreed to pay $300 million in
compensation to Brazil and reform its agricultural policies to bring them in line with WTO
rules.
Finally, unlike other IGOs involved in global economic governance, the WTO can be argued to
be effective in promoting the interests of all nations, as it operates on a “one country, one voteˮ
system, ensuring that it isnʼt dominated by developed Western countries.
One of the most significant examples of the WTO supporting developing countries is the
case of China. Since joining the WTO in 2001, China experienced a period of
unprecedented economic growth.
By gaining greater market access through reduced tariffs and being able to challenge
discriminatory trade practices within the WTO framework, China has become one of the
worldʼs leading exporters, particularly in sectors such as electronics, machinery, and
textiles.
Between 2001 and 2020, Chinaʼs GDP grew from approximately $1.3 trillion to $14.7
trillion, making it the second-largest economy in the world.

************************************************************************************************

For: Economic Global Governance Is Ineffective
On the other hand, it can be argued that the WTO has in fact been ineffective at promoting free
trade, as in recent years especially it has been unable to address major global trade disputes
and its rulings and rules are often ignored by powerful countries.
This has undermined its promotion of free trade, in particular the “most-favoured-nationˮ
principle it operates with, according to which no country can offer special trade
advantages, such as lower tariffs or preferential treatment, to one trading partner without
extending the same conditions to all other WTO members.
Although the Dispute Settlement Body DSB is one of the WTOʼs key mechanisms, its
enforcement ability has been shown to be weak in recent years. Over 20% of trade disputes
brought to the WTO in the last decade remain unresolved.
High-profile disputes, such as the USChina trade war, illustrate how countries have
increasingly bypassed WTO dispute resolution mechanisms in favour of unilateral actions
like tariffs, which contradict WTO rules.
In 2020, the WTO ruled that US tariffs on $360 billion of Chinese goods violated global
trading rules, but the United States rejected the ruling, further undermining the
organisationʼs ability to resolve disputes.
The Appellate Body, which reviews appeals, has also been hampered by the US
blocking new appointments, rendering it unable to fully function.
For example, in 2024 this meant that India could continue to impose tariffs on digital
services that violated WTO rules, as it appealed the decision to the DSB which wasnʼt
functional. The EU criticised this practice, which it called “appealing into the voidˮ.
This erosion of trust in the WTOʼs ability to enforce rulings has led to a rise in trade conflicts
and decreased compliance with its rules, diminishing its role as an arbitrator in global trade.
The WTO can also be argued to be ineffective as, while the WTO technically operates on a “one
country, one voteˮ principle, in practice, wealthier nations—particularly the United States and
European Union— exert disproportionate influence over trade negotiations.
Many developing countries struggle to have their voices heard, lacking the resources and
negotiating power to fully engage in complex, multi-layered trade discussions.
For example, during the Doha Round, developed countries pushed for greater access to
developing markets while resisting efforts to cut agricultural subsidies that harm poorer
nationsʼ farmers.
Additionally, issues critical to developing countries, such as technology transfer and
flexible intellectual property rights, are often sidelined in favour of trade rules that
benefit advanced economies.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, as with the IMF and World Bank, the WTOʼs purpose and presence is necessary for
global trade, however it is currently ineffective as the worldʼs most powerful economies are
ignoring its rules and mechanisms and therefore undermining its effectiveness.

Paragraph Three ➡ The G7 and The G20
Against: Economic Global Governance Is Effective
The G7 Group of Seven) and G20 Group of Twenty) are informal forums where the worldʼs
major economies come together to discuss and coordinate on global economic issues. Whilst
they donʼt have formal structures and legal frameworks, they are highly important as they bring
important world leaders together at annual summits to discuss and address key global
challenges.
It can be argued that they are effective as they bring together the worldʼs most influential
political and economic leaders who have the real decision-making power. This structure
allows for swift consensus on global challenges, ensuring that agreements reached at these
meetings have real influence on global policy decisions and lead to coordinated action
across nations.
For example, at the G7 Summit held in Apulia Italy in June 2024, the group agreed to
provide Ukraine with $50 billion in loans to support its war effort versus Russia; a key aim of
the alliance of Western states that the G7 represents. These loans were backed by profits
from frozen Russian assets held in bank accounts in G7 countries, the first time this had
been done.
This demonstrates importance of the G7 , as it allows world leaders with major power to
come together and make key decisions to promote the interests of the West against key
challengers like Russia and China.
The G7/G20 can also be argued to be highly effective an important as they place a crucial role
in coordinating responses to global economic crises, such as during the 2008 financial crisis
and COVID19 Pandemic, when they acted quickly to stabilise the global economy.
In response to the COVID19 pandemic, the G7 pledged over $20 billion to support the
development and distribution of vaccines, treatments, and diagnostics through mechanisms
like the COVAX initiative, aimed at ensuring equitable vaccine distribution.
The G7 Finance Ministers also worked closely to provide economic support to nations
struggling with the fallout from the pandemic. They led efforts to suspend debt
repayments for the worldʼs poorest countries through the Debt Service Suspension
Initiative DSSI.
Similarly, the G20 was highly important in responding to the 2008 financial crisis. At the
Washington Summit in November 2008, G20 leaders coordinated a global response,
pledging $5 trillion in stimulus packages to prevent further economic collapse
The G20ʼs collective efforts, including reforms to banking regulations and capital
requirements, are credited with stabilising the global economy and fostering recovery.

************************************************************************************************

For: Economic Global Governance Is Ineffective
On the other hand, it can be argued that the G7 and G20 are ineffective as they are informal
forums that lack the authority to enforce binding decisions. Agreements made at these summits
are often non-binding, leading to minimal real-world impact.
For example, while the G20 has committed to addressing climate change and other global
challenges, many of these promises remain unfulfilled due to a lack of mechanisms to
ensure compliance. For example, the promise to provide $100 billion annually in climate
finance to help developing countries was only met 2 years late in 2022.
Without enforceable commitments, these meetings often result in broad declarations
with little follow-through.
In recent years, both forums have also been ineffective in mediating/tackling key disputes
and issues, including the USChina trade war and the Russia-Ukraine War. This is largely
due to the inability for major countries on opposite sides of conflicts to find compromises.
For example, the 2024 G20 summit in Rio de Janeiro even failed to agree on any
statement condemning Russiaʼs invasion of Ukraine, as attempts by Western countries
to do so were blocked by China and Russia.
Another important argument that the G7 and G20 are ineffective is that they include the vast
majority of countries and only represent and serve the worldʼs richest.
The G7 includes only seven highly industrialised countries, while the G20 excludes over
80% of the worldʼs countries, including many small and developing nations.
This lack of representation undermines the legitimacy of these groups as forums for global
governance, as the decisions made naturally follow the interests of the worldʼs wealthier
nations.

************************************************************************************************

Judgement
Overall, the G7 and G20 can be argued to be effective, as they bring together those with the
real decision making power and can be highly important forums when tackling global financial
crisis. Though it is true that the non-binding nature of their agreements means they donʼt have
as much weight as they could, they are often highly important and on balance the G7 and G20
are still effective.

************************************************************************************************
************************************************************************************************

Overall Argument
In conclusion, economic global governance is ineffective. The WTO, IMF, World Bank, G7 and
G20 are all necessary institutions that play an important role, however they are currently
ineffective at serving the interests of the vast majority of the worldʼs countries, particularly the
worldʼs poorest countries.
The WTO has lost legitimacy in recent years due to the US and China bypassing its institutions
and ignoring its rules, whilst the World Bank and IMFʼs policies to promote austerity measures
and neoliberalism did far more harm to countries that were the subject of SAPs than they
helped. They continue to operate based on neoliberal assumptions are are therefore ineffective.
The G7/G20 are more effective, but they are arguably less important and also fail to represent
the vast majority of the population.
